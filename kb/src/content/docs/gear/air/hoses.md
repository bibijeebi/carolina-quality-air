Two completely different hose systems on every job. Mixing them up in conversation is common, so get the names straight:

| Hose | Size | Connects | Moves |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Collector hose** (neg-air tubing, flex) | 8" standard, some 10" and 6" | [Gas vac](/kb/gear/vacuum/meyer-gas-vac/) or [HyperVac](/kb/gear/vacuum/hypervac/) to the duct | Dirty air **out** of the system. Suction. |
| **Airline** | 3/8" | [Compressor](/kb/gear/air/compressors/) to the [whip, forward, reverse, or soaker](/kb/gear/air/air-tools/) | Compressed air **in**. Pressure. |

## Collector hose

Green spring-wire flex in sections, joined with quick connects or duct tape, clamped at the machine.

<dl class="facts">
<dt>Standard</dt><dd>8 inch. The HyperVac takes it through a 10-to-8 reducer collar.</dd>
<dt>Odd sizes</dt><dd>One 10" half-length with reducer, one 6" with reducer, one loose 8-to-10 reducer. Carry the reducers.</dd>
<dt>Where it lives</dt><dd>On the floor of every van. Bulk tubing in the trailer. About four sections in better shape in the box truck.</dd>
<dt>Condition</dt><dd>The worst gear in the fleet. Every van section carries tape repairs.</dd>
</dl>

**Why taped hose matters.** A leak on the suction side pulls air from the room instead of the duct. Every taped section is lost suction on every job. The August 30 catalog found two ends torn off their cuffs, the wire helix exposed, and cuff rings detached. The fix is new sections and cuffs from a duct cleaning supplier (Nikro, Meyer and similar), not big-box dryer hose. Count the box truck and trailer stock before buying.

**Field repair.** Duct tape on a torn reinforced hose might hold for a few minutes if you wrap it tight, spiral with overlap, extend 2 to 3 inches past the damage on both sides, and don't flex that section. It's a finish-this-run fix. Tag the section when you get back.

**Reach.** Shorter is better. The gas vac manual says the vacuum works better with less hose, and residential setups run about 50 to 75 ft. Details and sources on the [gas vac page](/kb/gear/vacuum/meyer-gas-vac/#how-it-works).

**Handling.**

1. Big loose loops or a figure eight. Corrugated hose has memory, and a kink becomes a permanent flat spot.
2. In an attic, tie it off to a beam. An untied hose snakes back down the access into the floor below.
3. Keep it off the gas vac's exhaust.
4. Check the cuffs on the van check. "Cuffs intact, no tape fixes" is on the sheet.

## Airlines

<dl class="facts">
<dt>Size</dt><dd>3/8 inch.</dd>
<dt>On the reels</dt><dd>About 200 ft of line on each van's powered reel.</dd>
<dt>Loose lines</dt><dd>Teal 3/8" line, about 100 ft, on a rib hook in Van 1. An orange line and a teal hose in the box truck.</dd>
<dt>Fittings</dt><dd>Brass quick-connects. A loose brass ball valve has been found on the ground twice. It's on the tether list.</dd>
</dl>

**Reach is a planning constraint.** On the hallway fan-coil job the blue airline couldn't cover the whole corridor. The crew did one side, then switched to the black line. Know how far each line reaches before you assign zones. See [JOB-006](/kb/jobs/write-ups/job-006-hallway-fan-coil-run/).

**Through a door.** Running an airline through a building's entry door can trip a door-held-open alarm. Don't defeat the sensor. Ask the building contact to put that door on test for the work window, or find another route.

## Hose reels

| Van | Reel |
|---|---|
| Van 1 | Reelcraft |
| Van 2 | Reelcraft |
| Van 3 | **Coxreels** |

One per van on the front wall. Van 3's is a Coxreels, not a Reelcraft, which matters when you order parts. Both makers build 12 V motor-driven air reels of this kind. Read the model off the reel label before ordering. Secure the line end before the van moves.

## Other hoses on the trucks

- **Pressure washer hose**, 50 ft with gun and wand. See [pressure washer](/kb/gear/wash/pressure-washer/).
- **Garden hoses.** The green one is kinked and spliced (retire it). The gray rubber one is good.
- **Airless paint hoses** for the encapsulation sprayer. Six paint-caked ones need a pressure test before anyone trusts them. The blue one with an electrical-taped junction is scrap: a taped joint on an airless line is not safe at pressure.
