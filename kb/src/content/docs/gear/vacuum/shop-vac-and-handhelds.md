:::caution[HEPA check]
None of these is on record as HEPA-rated at the discharge. Until the labels are checked, treat them as the "shop vac with a standard filter" row below: fine for the van and outdoors, not for exhausting inside an occupied space. _(HEPA rating of our Sharks unconfirmed.)_
:::

## The one rule

NADCA's guide spec: any vacuum that exhausts inside the building, **including hand-held and wet vacs**, needs HEPA filtration on its discharge, rated 99.97% at 0.3 micron.

| Machine | Inside an occupied space? |
|---|---|
| HEPA handheld or backpack vac | Yes. That's what it's for. |
| Shop vac with a standard filter | No. It blows the fine dust back into the house. Outside or in the van only. |
| Shop vac with an aftermarket HEPA cartridge | Only if the machine's label rates the discharge at 99.97% @ 0.3 micron. A HEPA filter in a leaky housing doesn't meet the rule. |

## What good looks like

For reference, Nikro's HEPA vacs (a common duct cleaning brand):

| Model | Airflow | Water lift | Filtration |
|---|---|---|---|
| Back-Pak BP00288 | 95 CFM | 88" | 99.97% @ 0.3 micron |
| 15-gal PD15110 | 115 CFM | 110" | 99.99% @ 0.3 micron |

Water lift is the number that matters for pulling debris out of a drain pan or a short branch. CFM is for moving air.

## Care

- Empty before it's full. A packed bag or canister drops suction and cooks the motor.
- Check the HEPA filter seal every time you open it. A HEPA filter with a gap around it is not HEPA.
- Follow the vac's manual for HEPA filter replacement.
