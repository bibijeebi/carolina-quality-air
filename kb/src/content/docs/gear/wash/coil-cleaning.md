Coils are thin aluminum fins. The whole game is getting the dirt out without folding the fins over, because folded fins block airflow worse than the dirt did.

## Why not the pressure washer

A typical pressure washer runs about 1,000 psi at 2 GPM or more. Coil makers want a fraction of that.

| Source | Limit |
|---|---|
| Carrier | "Avoid high-pressure washers, as the force can crush coil fins." Rinse around 100 psi or less, not over 100 to 150 psi on delicate fins. |
| EVAPCO | Air-handler coils around 125 psi at 0.5 GPM. Condenser coils 400 psi at around 3 GPM. |
| Goodway | Indoor coils 100 to 140 psi at 0.25 to 1 GPM. "Pressure washers just crush fins." Volume cleans, not pressure. |
| Trane (microchannel) | Nozzle 600 psi max, at least a 15° fan tip, 1 to 3 inches off the face, no more than 25° off square. |

That's why the pressure washer is for [grilles and registers](/kb/jobs/procedures/pressure-wash-grilles-and-registers/), never coils.

## The tools, best to worst

| Tool | What it is | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Coil jet** | Battery pump sprayer built for coils. SpeedClean's CoilJet CJ-125 runs 125 psi at 0.6 GPM, up to 12 tanks per charge. Goodway's CoilPro Jr. is the same idea. | Low flow won't overflow the condensate line on an indoor coil. Safe on microchannel. |
| **Soaker hose** | [Airline siphon sprayer](/kb/gear/air/air-tools/#the-soaker-hose-siphon-sprayer) dropped in a bucket. | Fast and even for putting chemical on. |
| **Pump sprayer** | Hand-pumped garden-style sprayer. | Always works. Slower, less even. It's what you use when the good tools are in the wrong van, which has happened. |

Also on the truck for coil work: **a lot of rags.** More than you think.

## How to do it

1. Cleaner on, per the label. Enzymatic cleaner mixed to dilution is the standard for evaporator coils. Diluted degreaser when the coil is greasy rather than dusty. See [coil cleaners](/kb/chemicals/coil-cleaners/).
2. Let it dwell per the label.
3. **Rinse against the airflow.** Spray from the leaving-air side back toward the entering side so the dirt goes out the way it came in, not deeper into the coil.
4. Keep the spray parallel with the fins.
5. On microchannel coils, **rinse every time, even "no rinse" products.** Trane says leftover cleaner "will significantly increase the possibility of corrosion damage."
6. Watch the drain pan and condensate line. Don't flood it.

The [steam cleaner](/kb/gear/wash/steam-cleaner/) is the no-chemical option.
