An ozone generator makes ozone (O₃), which oxidizes odor-causing organic material. It's the top of the [odor ladder](/kb/ladders/odor/): what you use when the smell is beyond what a deodorizer covers, like smoke or a building closed from the smell.

<dl class="facts">
<dt>Job</dt><dd>Heavy odor in an empty building or room.</dd>
<dt>Price</dt><dd>Ozone treatment lists at $250. Perry has comped it as goodwill, and when he does, the $250 gets said out loud. See <a href="/learning/pricing-doctrine.html">pricing doctrine</a>.</dd>

## House protocol

Perry's rule, given to customers:

1. **Nobody inside while it runs.** People, pets, and plants out.
2. When it's done, **open both doors and wait 15 minutes** before anyone goes back in.

Some makers call for much longer before re-entry (A2Z Ozone says 12 to 24 hours). Follow the manual for the machine you're running if it's stricter.

## What it doesn't do

EPA's assessment of ozone generators:

- At levels that are safe to breathe, ozone "has little potential to remove indoor air contaminants."
- It does **not** remove carbon monoxide, formaldehyde, or particles like dust and pollen. It's not a substitute for cleaning.
- It reacts with other chemicals indoors to form irritants.
- It damages **rubber, electrical wire coatings, and fabrics**, and it kills plants. Long runs in a space full of rubber gaskets or artwork are a risk.
