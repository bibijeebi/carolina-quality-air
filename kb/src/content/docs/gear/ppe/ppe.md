PPE on a duct job is matched to what you find, not worn by habit. Light dust is one level. Crawl spaces, soot and mold are another. Caustic coil cleaner is its own thing.

<dl class="facts">
<dt>On every van</dt><dd>Hard hat, bagged N95s, ear plugs, gloves, hi-vis vest. It's on the van gear check.</dd>
<dt>Laundry</dt><dd>Coveralls and rags go through the wash between jobs. The laundry basket and detergents ride in Van 1.</dd>
<dt>Short</dt><dd>Fall harnesses. The fleet has one, maybe two, for three vans. Two more are on the list.</dd>
</dl>

## When each goes on

| Situation | What we wear |
|---|---|
| General duct work, light dust | N95, gloves, long sleeves. |
| Attic or anywhere near fiberglass | Long sleeves and pants. Fiberglass on bare skin itches for days. |
| Crawl spaces | The suit. |
| Cutting and handling sheet metal | Leather gloves. Fresh-cut edges and hole-saw burrs open hands up. |
| Mixing or spraying coil cleaner | Chemical gloves and eye protection. Alkaline condenser cleaners burn like acid. See [coil cleaners](/kb/chemicals/coil-cleaners/). |
| Fogging | Mask and gloves. Don't breathe the fog. See [fogger](/kb/gear/air-treatment/fogger/). |
| Gas vac, compressor, pressure washer running | Ear plugs. |
| Heavy soot, visible mold, animal waste | Step up past an N95 per the hazard assessment, for example a half-face respirator with particulate cartridges, plus the suit and containment. Check with the person running the job first. See [hazards and stop-work](/kb/jobs/rules/hazards-and-stop-work/). |
| Lifts and open edges | Harness, tied off. |

## N95s: the rules even when they're optional

When an N95 is worn by choice and not required, OSHA's Appendix D to 1910.134 still applies. Its four points:

1. Read and follow the maker's instructions on use, care and limits.
2. Use a NIOSH-certified respirator for the contaminant you're dealing with.
3. Don't wear it into an atmosphere it isn't designed for. A dust respirator doesn't protect against gases, vapors, or very small particles of fumes or smoke.
4. Keep track of your own. Don't wear someone else's.

Filtering facepieces (dust masks) worn voluntarily are exempt from the written respirator program. Voluntary use of anything else, like a half-face respirator, needs the program elements that make sure the wearer is medically able to use it and that it's cleaned, stored and maintained. Required use brings in the full program. _(Whether CQA has a written respirator program is unconfirmed.)_

## Work clothes

- Boots with a stiff sole. You step on screws and sheet metal edges.
- Long sleeve shirt, work pants, knee pads if you have them.
