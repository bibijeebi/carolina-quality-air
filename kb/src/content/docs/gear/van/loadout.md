Three ProMaster vans, numbered 1, 2 and 3 with reflective numbers on the partition. The number follows the vehicle, not the gear. Behind them sit the box truck (the depot on wheels), the enclosed trailer (bulk 8 inch tubing and the long ladders) and Jeff's garage.

The rule for the vans: **each van is a self-contained job kit.** If a van can't finish a job alone, it's short something. Anything on the standard that's missing is a problem. Anything not on it is clutter until it earns a spot.

## Commonly forgotten

These are the items that have actually cost a crew time:

1. **The HyperVac power adapter or cord.** Lost most of a morning at the Durham County Courthouse when the only one was in the van that blew a tire. See [power cords and adapters](/kb/gear/van/power-cords-and-adapters/).
2. **The soaker hose and coil jet.** Rode off in another van on a two-story residential job. Coils got done with a pump sprayer. See [coil cleaning](/kb/gear/wash/coil-cleaning/).
3. **The second hole cutter.** With one, the whole crew balances around it. See [hole cutters](/kb/gear/tools/hole-saw-and-fairbairn-bit/).
4. **Charged batteries.** Packs turned up under seats and on tools in another van.
5. **Gas can spout caps.** They pop off and vanish. Now tethered with paracord.
6. **Rags.** More than you think.

## Two rules from the resets

- **Tether rule.** Any small part that detaches from its parent gets a paracord leash. Gas can spout caps first, then pressure washer tips, cord adapters, reducers, the brass ball valve.
- **Label rule.** Nothing gets a label until it has an ID in the master sheet. The label follows the row.

And one from the August 30 pull-out: **fuel, oxidizers and flammables never share a shelf.** Torch cylinders were found loose in a metal bowl and a jug of 12% peroxide sat beside the fuel cans. See [chemical storage](/kb/chemicals/storage-and-other-products/).

## The van gear check

The printed sheet in each van, by section:

1. **Machines and power.** Gas vac: fuel, oil, starts. Compressor: fuel, oil, relief valve pops. Fuel shutoffs off before transport. Gas can full, spout works. At least one extension cord. Batteries charged, charger aboard. Jumper cables.
2. **Hose and air.** Flex sections with intact cuffs (no tape fixes). Reducers (8" to 10", 6"). Air hose and quick-connects. Reel line secured.
3. **Rods, whips, brushes.** Rod quiver full (count it). At least two whip heads. Rotary brush heads. Drill brush attachment.
4. **Drill and fasteners.** Drill and impact. Nut setters. #2 Phillips bits. Screws, pop plug can, patches, foil tape, mastic. Snips, magnetic tray.
5. **Containment.** Clean drop cloths, poly roll, three door stops, heavy trash bags, clean rags, shoe covers.
6. **Chemicals.** Bio-Fresh, coil cleaner, Simple Green and sprayer, a spray bottle whose trigger works.
7. **Cleanup and verify.** Electric vac with the cup empty, DeWalt fan, dustpan and brush, charged headlamp or worklight, before and after photos.
8. **PPE.** Hard hat, N95s, ear plugs, gloves, hi-vis vest.
9. **Customer.** Duct Serviced stickers, yard sign, registers back on straight, access cuts patched, walkthrough done.
10. **Van.** Ladders strapped, nothing loose, wheel chocks, clean floor, fuel above half, keys accounted for.

Write what's short, broken or low on the bottom of the sheet and tell the person running the reset.

## Weekly reset

Sunday, whole fleet parked. Start charging batteries on arrival, then check each van against its sheet, restock consumables, pull broken gear (fix it on the spot or order the part the same day), put everything back in its home, photograph each van, and put the keys in the agreed spot. If the same item runs short three weeks running, the standing stock level changes.
