## Hoses in the stairwell

The hose route ran through a stairwell. The crew was told to mark it with safety orange where people would walk across it. All that was on hand was yellow non-adhesive caution tape.

The fix: caution tape is barrier tape. It is made to be tied off, not stuck down. Tie one end to the stair rail, run it across at about knee height above the hose crossing, tie the other end to the opposite rail or a door handle. That puts a visual stop in front of anyone coming down the stairs.

For floor marking on concrete, the right material is gaffer tape in orange or yellow. It comes up clean.

**Carry forward**: a roll of bright gaffer tape belongs in the van next to the caution tape. Any hose that crosses a walkway in an occupied building gets marked before work starts.

## The split airline hose

A braided airline hose failed mid-day. The outer rubber jacket was shredded in several places, the braid was exposed, and air was spurting from one spot.

- Tape will not make that hose safe. The jacket is what holds the pressure and it is gone.
- To finish one short task, a tight wrap of electrical tape under duct tape, running 2 to 3 inches past the damage on both sides, can hold for a few minutes. Keep that section still.
- After the job, the hose is done. Damage in several spots means the rest of it is close behind.

See [Air compressors](/kb/gear/air/compressors/) and [Air whip and forward nozzle](/kb/gear/air/air-tools/).

## Carry forward

1. Walk the hose route before you run hose. Stairwells and doorways in occupied buildings need a marked crossing.
1. A hose with a shredded jacket is tagged out at the van, not taped and sent back in.
