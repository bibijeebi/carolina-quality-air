## Why summer

Residence halls only get touched between terms. Empty building, no occupants to work around, and a hard deadline when students come back. That is why the days run long.

## Room-by-room work

Dorms are the highest unit-count-per-square-foot job type CQA does. Every room has its own unit. The work is not technically hard, it is a production line, which makes crew splitting and pre-staging the whole game. Same shape as [JOB-006 Hallway fan-coil run](/kb/jobs/write-ups/job-006-hallway-fan-coil-run/).

Per unit, as worked here:
1. Pull the face plate. Some screws are stripped. If the bit will not turn them, note the room and move on rather than wrecking the plate.
1. Clean the unit and coil. See [Coil cleaner and pump sprayer](/kb/gear/wash/coil-cleaning/).
1. New filter in.
1. **Let it sit before powering on.** The instruction on site was to give washed units time so water does not hit the electronics.
1. Power on and check airflow and buttons.

## The front door alarm

The air hose ran from the compressor outside through the dorm's front entrance. Holding that door open sets off a door-held-open alarm. That door contact reports to the building's security or fire panel.

Do not defeat the sensor. The fix is a phone call: whoever booked the job has the campus facilities contact, and facilities can put that door on test at the panel for the work window. Make that call before the first hose goes through a monitored door.

## Water from a dorm sink

Coil washing needs a hose, and a dorm room has a sink. Sink faucets use aerator threads, not garden hose threads.

- Unscrew the aerator. The spout is threaded one of two ways: female (the adapter's 15/16 inch-27 male end goes in) or male (the adapter's 55/64 inch-27 female end goes on).
- A **dual-thread faucet-to-garden-hose adapter** (Danco makes one) covers both and ends in a 3/4 inch male garden hose thread.
- Some institutional faucets have a recessed vandal-resistant aerator with nothing to grip. That takes an aerator key. They are sold in the same aisle.

Put one dual adapter and a key set in the van for every dorm job.

## The unit survey

Along with cleaning, each unit got a function check, and problems were reported to Jeff by room number. Findings from the second floor show what the survey catches:

| Finding | Examples |
|---|---|
| Dead, will not power on | 229, one hallway unit by the bathroom |
| Powers on, buttons dead | One hallway unit. Suspect the two-pin connector. |
| "Check filter" warning and no airflow | 213 |
| Clicking every couple of seconds | 240, 248, 258 |
| Sputtering or scratching, likely motor | 239 |
| Face plate screws stripped, unit likely missed | 242 |

Across the building, the broken list sorted into three failure modes: fully dead (229), panel partly lit but no response and no air (325), and powers on and takes settings but blows no air (213, 315, 329, 333, 350).

That list is a service lead for the customer. See [JOB-012](/kb/jobs/write-ups/job-012-ecu-clement-hall/) for how the report goes to Jeff.

## Carry forward

1. Bring the faucet adapter kit to every dorm.
1. Note room numbers as you go. A unit you skip or cannot fix is only useful to the customer if it is on the list.
