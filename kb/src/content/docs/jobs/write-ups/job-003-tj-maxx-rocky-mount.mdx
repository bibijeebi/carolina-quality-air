## Why overnight

Retail sales floor. You cannot run negative air machines and drag tube across a store that is open. Overnights on occupied commercial is the norm, and it is the same reason school work happens after 3pm.

## What to bring to an overnight

The shirts came from the prime contractor. On a sub job the prime hands one to everyone on site, and some primes collect them back at the end. They are not CQA shirts. Everything else was on the tech. What got bought on the way the first night:

- KN95 masks, a two-pack each
- Goatskin work gloves. Leather palm for sheet metal edges, cloth back so your hands do not cook. Nitrile-dipped gloves do not stop a duct edge.
- Safety glasses
- A 6-in-1 screwdriver and nut driver. The nut drivers matter because hex-head sheet metal screws are everywhere on duct connections.
- A utility knife

Shoes: closed-toe sneakers or tennis shoes, not work boots. Jeff's reasoning: you are walking all night, and if you are a hustler, running a little.

Also: a hoodie for the overnight, plus water and food, because nothing near the site is open.

Most tool stores are closed or closing by the time you leave for a 9 PM call. Buy gear the day before.

## The new person's role

Support work. Remove registers, manage hoses, move drop cloths, keep track of screws. Headlamps rarely came out on these first jobs.

## Shape of the job

Retail box store, so expect a rooftop unit feeding a supply trunk above the sales floor with branch runs to ceiling diffusers, and return either ducted or an open plenum return.

## Carry forward

1. Have your own PPE and a driver kit before an overnight. The van may not have spares for everyone.
1. Night work is short on stores and short on help. Anything you forgot stays forgotten until morning.
