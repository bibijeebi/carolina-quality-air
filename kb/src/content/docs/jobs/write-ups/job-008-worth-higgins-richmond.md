The big out-of-town run. CQA was working under Servpro as the general contractor, so the rules were not CQA rules.

## The building

Worth Higgins and Associates is a sheet-fed commercial printer. Printing, mailing, warehousing, fulfillment, variable data, digital publishing. Foil stamping, embossing, die cutting, automated gluing. They run 24 hours a day, six days a week.

That tells you exactly what is in the ducts before you open one: **paper dust and ink particulate**. Paper dust is fine, light, and everywhere, and it packs into returns the same way lint does at [JOB-014 Lenoir County Jail](/kb/jobs/write-ups/job-014-lenoir-county-jail/). Ink particulate makes it greasy rather than fluffy, so it clings instead of falling.

A plant that never shuts down also means the HVAC never gets a natural downtime window, which is why a contractor gets called instead of the in-house guys doing it on a weekend.

## Working under a GC

Different from a CQA-run job in two ways:
- Hard start and hard end times set by the GC, not finish-when-done.
- A hard lunch period, taken whether or not you were mid-run.

## Travel

The crew drove down and back more than once, some nights in a hotel, some nights driving back to Raleigh. Marcus rode along from Raleigh. Finishes were expected around 5 or 6pm. Actual days ran much later.

## Carry-forward

On any GC-run job, confirm the GC's start, end, and lunch rules and the travel plan before you leave. Expect days to run past the stated finish.
