## The building

Worth Higgins is a commercial printer: printing, direct mail, kitting and fulfillment, signage, wide format. The Park Central Dr facility is about 117,000 square feet, and they advertise 24/7 service.

That tells you what is in the ducts before you open one: **paper dust and ink particulate**. Paper dust is fine, light, and everywhere, and it packs into returns the same way lint does at [JOB-014 Lenoir County Jail](/kb/jobs/write-ups/job-014-lenoir-county-jail/). Ink particulate makes it greasy rather than fluffy, so it clings instead of falling.

Then there had been a fire, so there was **soot** through the system on top of that.

A plant that runs around the clock also means the HVAC never gets a natural downtime window, which is why a contractor gets called instead of the in-house guys doing it on a weekend.

## Working under a GC

Different from a CQA-run job:
- Hard start and hard end times set by the GC, not finish-when-done.
- A hard lunch period, taken whether or not you were mid-run.
- The crew's time went on the **Servpro timesheet**, one sheet for everyone.
- **Servpro built the containment.** Their crew put up the barriers, tape and white sheeting sectioning off work zones with zip or hook-and-loop doors. CQA's crew opened the door, stepped in, and worked inside the zone.
- Servpro had an OSHA safety officer walking the site. The client's forklift was on site but it was the client's, so nobody from CQA ran it.

That is two layers of containment on a soot job: the room-level barriers the restoration GC builds, and the system-level sealing CQA does inside the ductwork by closing off registers and protecting the air handler. See [Stage and protect](/kb/jobs/procedures/stage-and-protect/) and [Set the negative air](/kb/jobs/procedures/set-the-negative-air/).

## Working at height

The duct was above a drop ceiling at warehouse height. Access was from a **JLG 20MVL vertical mast lift**, a one-person push-around lift rented from Sunbelt. The flex hose was fed up into the duct from the lift platform.

A mast lift is a MEWP, same family as scissor and boom lifts. The operator needs to be trained on it. CQA later sent Benny through a Sunbelt lift course. See [Hazards and stop-work](/kb/jobs/rules/hazards-and-stop-work/).

## Grilles after a fire

The grilles got the normal wash with Simple Green, and most came clean. The ends of the grilles held baked-on soot that Simple Green would not move.

The step up: a non-acid alkaline condenser coil cleaner (Totaline Total Bright was what was on hand), mixed per the label in a pump sprayer, let dwell 5 to 10 minutes, rinsed off. Jeff's warning on it: it will burn you. Non-acid does not mean mild. It is caustic. Gloves and eye protection.

See [Coil cleaners](/kb/chemicals/coil-cleaners/) and [Pressure wash grilles and registers](/kb/jobs/procedures/pressure-wash-grilles-and-registers/).

## Travel

The crew drove up and back more than once, some nights in a hotel in the Glen Allen area, some days driven round trip from Raleigh. Crew was picked up in Raleigh on the way north. Jeff drove up himself on 4/10 to move crew. Meals on site were planned by Jeff, lunch and dinner. Finishes were expected around 5 or 6pm. Actual days ran much later.

## Carry forward

1. On any GC-run job, confirm the GC's start, end, and lunch rules and the travel plan before you leave. Expect days to run past the stated finish.
1. On a fire job, the restoration GC owns room containment. CQA still owns sealing the system.
1. Bring a caustic-rated plan for soot: coil cleaner, pump sprayer, gloves, glasses.
1. If a lift is on site, get trained on it before you need it.
