## What encapsulation actually is

Encapsulation is spraying a coating over the inside of **internally lined duct** after it has been cleaned. It is not painting bare sheet metal.

The trigger is **bare metal showing on the supply side** of lined duct. That means the liner is eroding off in spots. That is failing liner, and it is the moment to show the customer.

The alternative fix for failing liner is to tear the liner out and wrap the duct in external insulation. CQA subs that to an HVAC contractor, and it is expensive. Encapsulating in place keeps the whole job in house.

## Order of work

1. Clean first. Always. A coating over dirt seals the dirt in. See [Whip and forward a run](/kb/jobs/procedures/whip-and-forward-a-run/).
1. Photograph the liner condition, bare spots included.
1. Encapsulate with the sprayer, following the coating's label.
1. Photograph after.

## The sprayer

The paint sprayer CQA owns is known to clog. On this job it went easy. Clean it right after use, every time, so the next job gets the same result. It is only needed on encapsulation jobs, so one sprayer serves the fleet.

## Why the photos matter

The before-and-after set from this house is what gets shown to facilities people and property managers. A tech who can pull it up on a phone and say "call me when you see bare metal on a supply" is selling the next job.

## Carry forward

1. On any lined duct job, look for bare metal on the supply side and photograph it.
1. Flag it to the customer mid-job, with photos. Encapsulation is its own line on the ticket.
1. Clean first, coat second. No exceptions.
