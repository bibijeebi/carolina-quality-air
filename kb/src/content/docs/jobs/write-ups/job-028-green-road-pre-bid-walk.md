## What the walk found

- **Packaged units.** Three on the community center roof, gym units on the ground and roof. Both gym roofs are bare, so gym duct is side-fed long runs at height. That means a lift. See [Package unit](/kb/jobs/systems/package-unit/).
- **One gym is out.** Its equipment was replaced about four years ago and its ductwork is excluded from cleaning (Addendum 1).
- **The split system**, AC-4, is about 20 years old and stays. Its coils get cleaned and filters replaced (Addendum 2).
- **Fabric duct.** The in-scope gym has fabric duct. That scope is take it down, clean it by the manufacturer's instructions, and reinstall it. Brand not known to the engineer. Plan on reading the care tag on removal day.
- **Building will be vacant** during construction. Daytime work.
- **Duct cleaning is base bid item 2**, but the spec book has no duct cleaning section and names no standard. A sub quote that names NADCA ACR is the only document that defines the method.

## How a pre-bid walk is worked

1. Sign in. The sign-in sheet is the list of every contractor who might need the sub quote.
1. Count units yourself. Do not trust the drawings alone.
1. Look for what the drawings do not show: fabric duct, lift access, retained equipment.
1. Write questions for the engineer by the deadline. Get answers in an addendum, not a hallway conversation.
1. Build the estimate after the walk. See the [Green Road estimate](/estimates/green-road.html).

## Carry forward

1. On a public bid, the addenda are the scope. Read every one.
1. Fabric duct is a real line item: removal, cleaning, drying, reinstall, and a lift.
1. Retained equipment next to replaced equipment is where scope confusion lives. Ask in writing.
