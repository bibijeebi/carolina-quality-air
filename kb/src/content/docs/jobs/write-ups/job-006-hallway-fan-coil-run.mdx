Written from a recording of the on-site brief at the start of the shift, so it is the best record there is of how a crew actually gets split up.

## The layout as briefed

Duct comes down the hallway along one wall, turns, and runs a short leg. The unit being opened has a bathroom right next to it. Registers get pulled all down the hallway before anyone starts cleaning.

## Equipment call

Two vacuums, one at each end of the hallway, working toward each other. Two airlines, blue and black. The blue airline is short on reach, so it covers one side only, then the crew switches over to the black line for the rest.

See [Air whip and forward nozzle](/kb/gear/air/air-tools/).

## Crew split

Six bodies. Two on pulling and stacking registers, starting at opposite ends of the hallway and working back toward the middle. Two on cleaning the unit itself. The rest cutting access holes ahead of the cleaning crew.

The stated constraint: two hole cutters available. With one, the whole line balances around a single cutter and the cleaning crew waits. Cutting ahead of the cleaners is the ideal and it did not happen that day because there were not enough people.

Names heard: Will, David, Nate, Jack, and one heard as Jaw. _(name unconfirmed)_

## The unit was hot

Called out explicitly in the brief. The comfort kit went up with the crew:
- drinks, water, ice from the truck
- a DeWalt fan for the room
- Cool Jet coil cleaner
- a lot of rags
- two hole cutters

See [Coil cleaner and pump sprayer](/kb/gear/wash/coil-cleaning/).

## Method notes

Registers stack in one area rather than getting scattered, so nothing walks off and reinstall is fast. The backs of the registers are assumed dirty even if the fronts were wiped, so check the back side before calling one clean. Access panels on these units fold out from the top once you push the top in, same as a residential filter door.

Site not recorded: Wilson, Craven, or somewhere else. _(unconfirmed)_
