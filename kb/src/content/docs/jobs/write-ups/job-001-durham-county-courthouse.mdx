Crew: Jeff running it, plus Thomas, Andrew, Nate, Jack, Bryson, Benny, and one more heard as Hi. Will's van blew a tire on the way and did not make it, and that van had the adapter plug for the portable vacuum.

## The equipment problem

That missing adapter is the whole logistics story of the day. It was a 20 A to 15 A adapter for the portable vacuum's cord. A tech drove from downtown to a big-box store to find one. The store app showed the right part as ship-to-store only, not on the shelf. Big-box stores rarely stock NEMA locking adapters on the shelf, so an electrical supply house is the better same-day bet. A replacement was found, and an Amazon three-pack of spares went to Jeff afterward so it would not happen again. The rule: the tool that strands a crew is never the expensive one.

See [Electric portable vacuum](/kb/gear/vacuum/hypervac/).

## The system

<p class="from">From <a href="/kb/jobs/systems/ptac-and-fan-coil/">PTAC and fan coil</a></p>

Returns and supplies both came off above the ceiling tiles. Because the units were in-wall and inside the building, the truck was the wrong tool. Short accessible runs, no reason to drag 200 feet of hose from the street. Portable was correct.

## The work

1. Trace the ductwork off each unit to a spot you can actually reach
1. Find an existing access or cut one
1. Feed the negative pressure hose in with the agitator on the end, the whip with the noodles, and knock the debris off the walls
1. Pull it into the portable collection unit
1. Move to the next unit, repeat across the building

How the access went in: find the spot on the duct run off each PTAC, cut an 8 inch hole with a drill-driven circle cutter, and on insulated runs cut the insulation back in a square first. Then fish the whip from the access back toward the PTAC. Jeff did most of the access cutting. New techs pulled and cleaned vents and ran for parts. See [Cut an 8 inch access](/kb/jobs/procedures/cut-an-8-inch-access/).

Jeff used the word "plenum" all day, for the returns and supplies above the ceiling tiles. It is one of the first words you will hear. See [Glossary](/kb/reference/glossary/).

## Why the method was right

Source removal cleaning is negative air plus mechanical agitation, and that is the NADCA-compliant method. Choosing portable over truck for in-wall units was the right call, not a shortcut.

## Carry-forward

Cutting access is normal, not damage. Good crews cap holes with sheet metal access doors so the next cleaning does not need a new cut. See [Patch a sheet metal access](/kb/jobs/procedures/patch-a-sheet-metal-access/).
