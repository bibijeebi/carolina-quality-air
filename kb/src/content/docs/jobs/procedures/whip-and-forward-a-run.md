Two tools, one rule.

- **[Whip](/kb/gear/air/air-tools/)** with the noodles or skipper balls: mechanical agitation. Knocks the debris off the duct wall.
- **Forward nozzle**, the air pusher on the [compressor](/kb/gear/air/compressors/) airline: directional air. Moves the loosened debris.

**The rule**: agitate, then push toward the negative air. Which one you pick up first only depends on where you are standing relative to the suction. There is no supply-side method and a different return-side method.

1. Start at the **far end** of a run and work back toward your negative air access.
1. **Branches before the trunk.** Whipping the trunk first and then the branches means you dumped branch debris into a trunk you already called clean.
1. Feed in, agitate, forward, retract. Repeat down the run.
1. On **internally lined** duct, do not whip hard. The liner releases fiber. Air washing is the right tool there.
1. Watch the machine. If the pull drops off, you have a kink, a clogged filter, or a leak at a joint.

**Hose handling**: loose figure-eight or big loops. Corrugated hose has memory, and a kink becomes a permanent flat spot that costs you airflow. Careful over-under is not needed on a 6 inch corrugated hose.
