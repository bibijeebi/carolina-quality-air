Standard access hole in insulated sheet metal duct. Round trunk, rectangular trunk, or plenum, same method.

1. **Score the insulation on three sides only**, in a square, and roll the flap back. Three sides, not four, so it folds back and re-seats when you patch.
1. **Drill a pilot hole** dead center of where the access goes.
1. **[Hole saw](/kb/gear/tools/hole-saw-and-fairbairn-bit/) on the pilot, run clockwise, and go slow.** Speed leaves knurled burr edges on the sheet metal that will open your hand up.
1. Check the cutter is set to the size you actually want before you start. An oversized setting fights you the whole way through.
1. Deburr the lip if it is rough.

**Sizes**: 8 inch is the standard access. 4 inch when you need to reach a hand in, for example to fish out a dropped bit. 1 inch fairbairn-bit or step-bit holes for whip access along a trunk, which get pop plugs rather than a patch.

## Where to cut

- **Use what is already there first.** Registers, existing access doors, the plenum. Every hole you do not cut is a hole you do not patch. The NADCA guide spec says the same: work through existing openings where possible.
- **The negative air hooks up at an 8 inch access**, usually in the plenum, as close to the air handler as you can get. A 1 inch hole will not take the hose.
- **Reach decides spacing, not a tape measure.** Figure roughly 12 feet of good whip reach from one access. A long straight run can go further by adding airline extensions, as long as the compressor and negative air keep up.
- **Every 90 degree turn needs its own hole.** The whip will not track around the corner. Push one direction, and the next leg, however short, gets a new access.
- **On commercial rectangular trunk above a drop ceiling**, pull the tile, then cut. Square patch over the round hole.

## Where not to cut

- **Never in flex duct.** ACR 2021 4.2.9. Work flex from its ends. See [Flex duct](/kb/jobs/systems/flex-duct/).
- **Not where the opening will weaken the duct or change the airflow.** ACR 2021 4.2.1 and 4.2.3: a service opening must not degrade the structural, thermal, or functional integrity of the system or restrict the airflow.
- **Not somewhere you cannot close it.** ACR 2021 4.2.2: openings are made so they can be properly closed. Check you have room to set a patch and drive screws before you cut.
- **Fiberglass duct board and lined duct** follow extra rules. See [Duct board and lined duct](/kb/jobs/systems/duct-board-and-lined-duct/).

Size matters for verification too. ACR 2021 4.7.1: openings must be large enough for mechanical cleaning and to allow cleanliness verification.

Every hole gets patched. See [Patch a sheet metal access](/kb/jobs/procedures/patch-a-sheet-metal-access/) and [Pop-plug a bit hole](/kb/jobs/procedures/pop-plug-a-bit-hole/).
