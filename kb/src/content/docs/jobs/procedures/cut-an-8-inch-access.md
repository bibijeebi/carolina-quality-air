Standard access hole in insulated sheet metal duct. Round trunk, rectangular trunk, or plenum, same method.

1. **Score the insulation on three sides only**, in a square, and roll the flap back. Three sides, not four, so it folds back and re-seats when you patch.
1. **Drill a pilot hole** dead center of where the access goes.
1. **[Hole saw](/kb/gear/tools/hole-saw-and-fairbairn-bit/) on the pilot, run clockwise, and go slow.** Speed leaves knurled burr edges on the sheet metal that will open your hand up.
1. Check the cutter is set to the size you actually want before you start. An oversized setting fights you the whole way through.
1. Deburr the lip if it is rough.

**Sizes**: 8 inch is the standard access. 4 inch when you need to reach a hand in, for example to fish out a dropped bit. 1 inch fairbairn-bit holes for whip access along a trunk, which get pop plugs rather than a patch.

Every hole gets patched. See [Patch a sheet metal access](/kb/jobs/procedures/patch-a-sheet-metal-access/) and [Pop-plug a bit hole](/kb/jobs/procedures/pop-plug-a-bit-hole/).
