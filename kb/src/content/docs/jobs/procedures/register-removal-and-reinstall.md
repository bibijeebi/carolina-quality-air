Registers, grilles, and diffusers are the one part of the job the customer actually looks at. They come off early and go back on last.

## Tools

A 6-in-1 screwdriver with nut drivers covers most of them: Phillips, flat, and the hex-head sheet metal screws. Leather work gloves. The edges on a stamped grille cut.

## Pulling them

1. **Pull ahead of the cleaners.** On a hallway or a dorm, two people start at opposite ends and work back toward the middle. See [JOB-006 Hallway fan-coil run](/kb/jobs/write-ups/job-006-hallway-fan-coil-run/).
1. **Stack them in one area.** Scattered registers get stepped on, walk off, and slow reinstall to a crawl.
1. **Keep the screws.** Lost screws mean a parts run.
1. **Track where each one came from** when they are not identical.
1. **Note anything already broken, by room.** Stripped screws, a face plate that will not come off, a cracked frame. Example: a unit face plate at ECU Garrett Hall had both left screws stripped and would not turn with the bit driver. That went into the room notes for Jeff.

## Cleaning them

- **Lay them out on a tarp.** Not on the customer floor.
- **Check the back side.** Fronts get wiped by janitorial. Backs never do. Assume they are dirty even if someone wiped the fronts.
- **Wash.** Simple Green all-purpose is the normal cleaner. When the count is high, pressure wash. See [Pressure wash grilles and registers](/kb/jobs/procedures/pressure-wash-grilles-and-registers/).
- **Straighten bent grilles.** A crushed grille restricts airflow. Remove, clean, straighten, reinstall.

## Putting them back

1. **Nothing goes back on until the run behind it is clean.** An open register is also an access point.
1. **Dry before reinstall.**
1. **Same opening, same orientation.** ACR 2021 section 4.9: registers, grilles, and diffusers are removed if possible, cleaned, and "shall be restored to their previous position."
1. Every screw back in.
