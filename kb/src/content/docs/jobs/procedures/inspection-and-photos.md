Photos are the proof of the work. They protect us if something is questioned later, and they are the basis for any upsell.

## Before photos

1. **Phone up in the hole.** Once an access is cut or a register is off, put the phone camera into the duct and shoot the inside before the whip goes in.
1. **Borescope when the phone will not reach.** Around a turn or down a long run.
1. Shoot anything already damaged before you touch it. See [ACR section 4.24](#what-nadca-says): pre-existing damage is documented and brought to the client.

## After photos

Same spots, same angles, after cleaning. A before and after pair from the same access is what sells the job and closes arguments.

## What NADCA says

ACR 2021 section 5 sets how "clean" is verified. Verification is done **immediately after cleaning and before the system runs**.

| Method | What it is | Used on |
|---|---|---|
| Method 1, visual inspection | Look at it. Clean means "free from non-adhered substances and debris." If it passes visually, nothing more is needed. | Porous and non-porous |
| Method 2, surface comparison test | HEPA contact vacuum with a 2.5 in nylon brush, four passes, then compare the surface before and after. No significant visible change means clean. | Porous: duct liner, duct board |
| Method 2, NADCA Vacuum Test | A template and a filter cassette on a calibrated pump, sent to a lab. Pass is **0.75 mg per 100 cm² or less**. | Non-porous: sheet metal |

Method 2 only comes out when the visual is inconclusive or disputed. The surface for a vacuum test must be dry and the air handler off.

**The "wipe test"**: running a white glove or rag along the duct is a quick field check. It is not an ACR method. It is part of looking. _(any CQA wipe routine is unconfirmed)_

Two other ACR points that matter in the field:
- Visual observation of a coil "can be misleading." ACR recommends a static pressure drop reading across the coil before and after cleaning. (4.11.6)
- ACR recommends photos be part of the post-project documentation, and a vacuum test requires the lab results in the file. (5.6)

Verification checks for particulate. It does not test for mold or hazardous materials. (5.0)

## Room-by-room unit reports

On dorm and PTAC work Jeff sometimes has us check every unit after cleaning and report what does not work. Format that works for him: one line per room, what is wrong, nothing else.

```
10th floor:
1019: not blowing air
1040: locked, couldn't check
1008, 1012: constant humming
1017: rattle in the fan or motor
```

- Only report what he does not already know. Units that work are left off.
- Group by failure when there are a lot: dead, powers on but no air, noise.
- A locked room is a finding. Write it down.
- Watch voice-to-text. It turns room numbers into times.
