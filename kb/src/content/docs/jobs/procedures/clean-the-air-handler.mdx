The air handler is the last thing you clean, because it sits between the supply and return sides and catches whatever either side knocks loose.

What each section of the unit is, in airflow order, and what gets dirty: [Air handler components](/kb/jobs/systems/air-handler-components/).

1. **Power off** before the panel comes off. See [Safety and PPE](/kb/jobs/rules/safety-and-ppe/#power-and-lockout).
1. Pull the side panel off the air handler.
1. **Look at the coil and the drain pan before you spray.** Standing water, slime, or growth in the pan is a finding. Photograph it.
1. Mix the **[enzymatic coil cleaner](/kb/gear/wash/coil-cleaning/)** and load the pump sprayer, or run the [soaker hose](/kb/gear/air/air-tools/) off the airline if you have it. The airline-fed soaker or Cool Jet is faster and more even. The pump sprayer is the backup.
1. **Spray the evaporator coils**, a couple of passes, and let it work.
1. **Wipe the blower housing** exterior in place. It does not need to come out for a standard clean.
1. Reinstall the panel.
1. **New filter in.** Replacing the filters is part of the air handler job. A unit left with no filter loads the coil you just cleaned.

## In-wall units: let them sit

On PTAC and window units, the coil wash puts water inside a cabinet full of electronics. On the ECU dorm jobs the instruction was to **let the units sit before turning them back on** so the water does not get into the controls. Check each has a filter, then power up and confirm it blows air. See [PTAC and fan coil](/kb/jobs/systems/ptac-and-fan-coil/).

## What the standard adds

ACR 2021 4.11 on coils:
- A **visual inspection of the coil and drain pan** comes first and decides dry (Type 1) or wet (Type 2) cleaning. Suspect growth on the coil or pan means wet cleaning.
- Coil cleaning needs access to **both the upstream and downstream sides** of the coil. If you cannot get both, removal or replacement may be required.
- Wet cleaning includes the **drain pan and drain line: cleaned, flushed, and drainage tested before and after.**
- Cleaners follow the label and the coil maker, and ACR recommends solutions **as close to pH neutral as possible**. Nothing that erodes the fins.
- Looking at a coil can mislead. ACR recommends a **static pressure drop reading across the coil before and after** to show the cleaning worked.
- **Electric heat strips**: de-energize, lock out and tag out. Non-corrosive detergent only, rinse free of chemicals, dry before re-energizing.
- Wet cleaning water is captured and disposed of per local, state, and federal rules (4.6.2).

See [Coil cleaner and pump sprayer](/kb/gear/wash/coil-cleaning/).

The **condenser** coil does not get touched. It is not in the airstream. See [What is in scope](/kb/jobs/rules/what-is-in-scope/).
