Dryer vent cleaning is one of the services CQA sells alongside duct cleaning. It is a separate system from the HVAC ductwork: one dryer, one duct, straight to the outdoors. Nothing else is supposed to share it.

<dl class="facts">
<dt>Why it matters</dt><dd>USFA: failure to clean was the leading factor in home clothes dryer fires, 31%, 2018 to 2020.</dd>
<dt>Duct</dt><dd>4 in metal, smooth inside, at least 0.0157 in (28 gauge).</dd>
<dt>Max length</dt><dd>35 ft from the transition connection to the outlet, minus fitting allowances, unless the dryer maker says otherwise.</dd>
<dt>Transition</dt><dd>One piece, UL 2158A listed, 8 ft max, never concealed.</dd>
<dt>Termination</dt><dd>Outdoors. Backdraft damper. No screen. 3 ft from openings unless the dryer maker says otherwise.</dd>
</dl>

Code references below are the 2021 IRC model text, section M1502. North Carolina adopts its own edition of the residential code. Check the edition that applies to the house. _(NC edition differences unconfirmed)_

## The run, dryer to outside

| Part | Code requirement (IRC 2021) | Common defect to call out |
|---|---|---|
| Transition (dryer to wall) | Single length, listed and labeled to UL 2158A, not over 8 ft, not concealed in construction (M1502.4.3) | White vinyl or foil accordion hose, crushed behind the dryer |
| Duct | Metal, smooth interior, 4 in nominal (M1502.4.1). Supported every 12 ft or less. Male end in the direction of airflow. Joints sealed and mechanically fastened. No screws sticking in more than 1/8 in (M1502.4.2) | Long screws through the joints. Lint hangs on every one. |
| Length | 35 ft max, reduced by each fitting per Table M1502.4.6.1 (a 4 in radius mitered 90 counts as 5 ft), unless the dryer maker's instructions set it (M1502.4.6) | Long attic runs to the roof with several elbows |
| Label | Over 35 ft equivalent length needs a permanent label within 6 ft of the duct connection (M1502.4.7) | No label on a long run |
| Booster | Domestic booster fans prohibited. A dryer exhaust duct power ventilator listed to UL 705 is allowed, installed per its instructions (M1502.4.4, M1502.4.5) | Unlisted inline fan |
| Termination | Outside the building, backdraft damper, **no screen**, 3 ft from openings including ventilated soffits unless the dryer maker says otherwise, at least 12.5 sq in open area (M1502.3) | Screened or bird-guarded cap packed with lint. Vent ending in the attic or soffit. |
| Independence | Separate from every other exhaust system (M1502.2). Not into an attic, soffit, ridge vent, or crawlspace (M1501.1) | Tied into a bath fan duct. Dumping into the attic. |

## Terminations

- **Wall hood**: the easy one. Flap should swing free when the dryer runs.
- **Roof cap**: long run up through the attic. Roof work is a [ladder and fall exposure](/kb/jobs/rules/safety-and-ppe/#ladders) job. Screens and bird nests show up here.
- **No termination found**: if nobody can find where it comes out, it may end in the attic or a soffit. Find it before you push lint anywhere.

## Lint and fire

Lint is fuel. Any run that is long, has elbows, has screws in the airstream, or ends at a screen collects it faster. The same physics fills jail returns with compressed lint discs. See [JOB-014 Lenoir County Jail](/kb/jobs/write-ups/job-014-lenoir-county-jail/).

## The standard

NADCA's document for dryer exhaust is the **DEDP** (Dryer Exhaust Duct Performance) standard: a checklist of performance measurements for a residential single-dryer exhaust duct system, including a static pressure test of the duct.
