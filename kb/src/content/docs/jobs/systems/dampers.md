A damper is a blade or set of blades in the airstream that sets, changes, or stops airflow. Some are there for comfort. Some are there to keep a fire in one room. They look alike through an access hole, so learn the tells.

**The two rules:**
1. **Mark it, restore it.** ACR 2021 4.8: "Dampers and any air-directional mechanical devices shall have their position marked prior to cleaning and shall be restored to their marked position after cleaning."
1. **Life-safety dampers stay in service.** Nothing goes through a fire or smoke damper. Never remove, trip, reset, pin open, or wire open one. Never disturb a fusible link, actuator, or its wiring. ACR 4.10: cleaning "shall not impair, alter or damage any smoke and fire detection equipment."

## The types

| Type | What it does | How it moves | How you spot it | Crew rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Manual volume / balancing damper** | Sets a fixed airflow to a branch during test and balance | By hand, locked with a quadrant | Handle and locking quadrant on the outside of a branch near the takeoff. Drawing: "VD," slash with a handle. | Photograph or scribe the position. Put it back exactly. |
| **Motorized control damper** | Opens and closes on a controls signal: outside air, return air, relief, zone dampers | Actuator | Actuator on the duct or unit, wires or tubing to it. Drawing: "MOD." | Mark position. Do not force against the actuator. |
| **VAV box damper** | Varies air to one zone | Actuator driven by the box controller | Inside the VAV box | See [VAV boxes](/kb/jobs/systems/vav-boxes/). |
| **Backdraft (gravity) damper** | Lets air out one way, stops it coming back in | Airflow and gravity or a counterweight, no actuator | Exhaust fan outlets, dryer and bath fan wall caps, intakes. Drawing: "BDD." | Make sure it swings free when you are done. |
| **Fire damper** | Closes to stop fire spreading through a rated wall, floor, or shaft | Heat. Usually a spring or gravity with a **fusible link** that melts. | At a rated wall or floor penetration, with an access door. Drawing: "FD," blade symbol at a heavy rated-wall line. | Access each side. Nothing through it. Leave the link alone. |
| **Smoke damper** | Closes to stop smoke moving through a smoke barrier | Actuator, on a smoke detection signal | Actuator and wiring at a smoke barrier or in an engineered smoke control system. Check the legend for its tag. | Same as fire damper, plus hands off the actuator and wiring. |
| **Combination fire/smoke** | Both jobs in one | Actuator plus a heat-responsive device | Actuator and a heat sensor or link at a rated barrier. Drawing: "FSD." | Same as smoke damper. |
| **Ceiling radiation damper** | Heat barrier where a duct or diffuser goes through a rated ceiling membrane | Heat | Above a diffuser in a rated ceiling | Same as fire damper. |

## Blade styles

Greenheck: **parallel blades** all turn the same way and are "typically used when the damper operates in two positions, open or closed." **Opposed blades** turn opposite each other and are "typically used on dampers that modulate airflow." Balancing dampers are set once. Greenheck: they "are not intended for use in positive shut-off or automatic control applications."

## Fire and smoke dampers, what the codes say

These are not ours to test. Knowing the rules tells you why we do not touch them.

- **Listings.** Fire dampers are tested to UL 555, smoke dampers to UL 555S, and combination dampers to both (UL Solutions).
- **Fire dampers** "close automatically when heat is detected" where ducts pass through fire walls, fire barriers, fire partitions, horizontal assemblies, and shaft enclosures. Fusible links are certified to UL 33 (UL Solutions).
- **Smoke dampers** operate "automatically, controlled by a smoke detection system" and are designed to close against fan pressure (UL Solutions).
- **Testing** is done under NFPA 80 and NFPA 105: 1 year after acceptance, then every 4 years, every 6 years in a hospital (UL Solutions). Testing a link-type damper means pulling the link, confirming full closure, resetting, and reinstalling the link (NFPA 80 19.4.4.1, via the Seattle Fire Department acceptance form). That is the inspector's job, not ours.
- **Access.** Smoke damper access panels are labeled "Smoke Damper" in letters at least 1/2 in high, and insulation may not hide an access panel without a label showing where it is (NFPA 105 7.3.2.2). Dampers with links or internal operators get an access door or a removable duct section (NFPA 105 7.3.2). **A labeled access door on a duct is your tell that a damper is behind it.**

## On the job

1. **Find them on the walk and on the drawing first.** Rated walls are drawn with a heavy line, and FD/FSD tags sit where ducts cross them. See [Reading mechanical drawings](/kb/jobs/systems/reading-mechanical-drawings/).
1. **Plan access on both sides** of every fire and smoke damper. The damper is a wall for your whip and your airflow. Cut an access each side and treat each side as its own run.
1. **Do not open a damper access door to push tools through.** Look, photograph, close it back up. _(CQA practice on using damper access doors is unconfirmed, ask the lead)_
1. **Found one closed or with a dropped link?** Do not reset it. Photograph it, note location, tell the lead. It goes in the report.
1. **Found one stuck, rusted, or blocked by debris?** Same: photograph and report. You can vacuum loose debris near it without working the blades. _(unconfirmed)_
1. **Balancing and control dampers:** photograph the position before you start. A marker line across the quadrant makes it easy to put back. Restore at close-out. See [Close out](/kb/jobs/procedures/close-out/).

**If you trip a link, you own the repair.** Treat it as a stop-and-tell-the-lead moment.
