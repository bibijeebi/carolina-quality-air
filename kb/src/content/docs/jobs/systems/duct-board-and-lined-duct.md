Two different things that follow the same rules because the airstream touches fiberglass.

| | Duct board | Lined duct |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | Rigid insulation board, usually fiberglass, with a facing that is normally the vapor barrier. The board is the duct. | Sheet metal duct with insulation, usually fiberglass, applied to the **inside**, for heat and sound |
| Where you see it | Residential plenums and trunks | Commercial trunks |
| How you know | Foil or kraft facing outside, no metal | Metal outside, insulation surface inside when you look in the access |

Do not confuse lined duct with **wrapped** duct: insulation on the **outside** of the metal. Wrapped duct is bare metal inside and cleans like any sheet metal. It is the wrap you score on three sides and roll back when you [cut an access](/kb/jobs/procedures/cut-an-8-inch-access/).

## How to clean it

ACR calls fibrous glass liner and duct board **porous**. That drives everything:

1. **Dry only.** ACR 2021 4.4: cleaning agents or water "shall never be applied to" fibrous glass or other porous components. No coil cleaner overspray, no pressure washing, no wet wipe.
1. **Easy agitation.** ACR 2021 4.17.1: the cleaning method must not create "abrasions, breaks, or tears" in the liner or board. On lined duct, do not whip hard. Air washing with the forward nozzle is the right tool. EPA's guidance calls for soft-bristled brushes on duct board.
1. **Negative air on the whole time**, same as anything else.

## Cutting into it

- ACR 2021 4.2.8.1: an access cut in fiberglass must be made and closed so there are **no exposed fiberglass edges** in the airstream.
- ACR 2021 4.2.8.2: any fiberglass removed for the opening is repaired or replaced **with like material of the same thickness**, so there is no break in the R-value or vapor barrier.
- Duct board closures use UL 181-listed tape or mastic per NAIMA practice (NADCA guide spec). A screwed sheet metal patch is a sheet metal closure. _(the CQA method for patching duct board is unconfirmed)_

## Fogging and chemicals

**EPA: "No products are currently registered by EPA as biocides for use on fiberglass duct board or fiberglass lined ducts."** Before you [fog](/kb/jobs/procedures/fog-the-system/) a duct board or lined system, read the product label for the surfaces it allows. If the label does not list them, it does not go on them. Tell the lead.

## When to replace instead

- **Black particulate coming out of diffusers** can mean the liner is breaking down. Look at the liner first. _(field rule of thumb, not an ACR rule)_
- ACR 2021 4.19: when fiberglass is damaged, deteriorating, delaminating, or friable and cleaning or resurfacing cannot restore it, **replacement is recommended**.
- Wet or moldy fiberglass: EPA says mold-contaminated fiberglass cannot be effectively treated and should be replaced. ACR 2021 4.16 recommends removing and replacing porous materials with mold growth. That is a [stop-work call](/kb/jobs/rules/hazards-and-stop-work/), not a cleaning.
- Resurfacing or coating (ACR 4.18) is an option only after proper mechanical cleaning and only if the surface will hold the coating. EPA does not recommend routine use of sealants to encapsulate contaminants.

## Verifying it

Visual first. If disputed, porous surfaces get the surface comparison test, not the NADCA Vacuum Test. See [Inspection and photos](/kb/jobs/procedures/inspection-and-photos/).
