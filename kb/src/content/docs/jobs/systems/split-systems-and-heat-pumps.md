Most houses we clean are one of four setups. Figure out which one in the first five minutes, because it tells you where the coil, the filter, and the return plenum are.

| Setup | Outdoors | Indoors | Heat | Where you find it in NC |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Split AC + gas furnace** | Condenser: compressor, outdoor coil, fan | Furnace with the evaporator coil in a box on top (upflow) or below | Gas or propane burner and heat exchanger | Crawlspace, closet, basement |
| **Split heat pump + air handler** | Heat pump outdoor unit: same parts plus a reversing valve | Air handler: coil, blower, often electric heat strips | Heat pump, backup strips | Attic, closet, crawlspace |
| **Package unit** | The whole system in one cabinet | Nothing but ducts | Built in: electric coils or gas | On a slab beside the house, or on a roof |
| **Ductless mini-split** | Outdoor unit | Wall or ceiling head with its own fan and coil | Heat pump | Garages, additions, rooms with no ducts |

## Split systems

The outdoor cabinet holds the compressor and condenser coil. The indoor cabinet holds the evaporator coil, and the furnace or air handler moves the air through the ducts (Central Electric Cooperative). Two copper refrigerant lines join them. The larger, insulated one is the suction line. _(line identification: general practice)_

**Where things are on an upflow furnace:** return comes into the side or bottom through the filter rack, the blower at the bottom pushes up through the heat exchanger and then the evaporator coil, into the supply plenum on top. The box on top is supply. The box on the side or bottom is return.

**Attic air handler:** one big return, often one flex run from a hall ceiling grille, and a supply trunk. See [Attic air handler](/kb/jobs/systems/attic-air-handler/).

## Heat pumps

A heat pump moves heat instead of making it. DOE: "In heating mode, liquid refrigerant in the outside coil removes heat from the air and evaporates into a gas. The indoor coil releases heat from the refrigerant as it condenses back into a liquid." A reversing valve near the compressor flips the flow for cooling and for **defrosting the outdoor coil** in winter.

What that means for a duct tech:

- **The indoor coil works year round.** In cooling it is wet. In heating it is hot. Either way it is in the airstream and it is what we clean.
- **Defrost is normal.** A heat pump outdoor unit steaming on a cold morning is defrosting, not broken.
- **Backup heat.** Electric heat strips in the air handler, or a gas furnace in a dual-fuel setup (DOE). Electric strips get de-energized and locked out before any cleaning near them, and only non-corrosive detergent, rinsed and dried before power goes back (ACR 4.11.8).
- **Airflow matters more than people think.** DOE: about 400 to 500 cfm per ton of cooling capacity, and the common performance problems are "low airflow, leaky ducts, and incorrect refrigerant charge." A dirty filter, coil, or blower eats into that airflow.

Most heat pumps are split systems, "one coil inside and one outside." Packaged heat pumps have both coils and the fan outdoors (DOE).

## Package units

One outdoor cabinet, supply and return ducts come through the wall or roof to it (Central Electric Cooperative). Negative air and the air handler phase both happen outside. See [Package unit](/kb/jobs/systems/package-unit/). JOB-013 had both on one house: attic air handler upstairs, package unit downstairs. See [JOB-013](/kb/jobs/write-ups/job-013-two-story-residential-attic-ahu-and-package-unit/).

## Ductless mini-splits

No duct network. The indoor head is its own fan and coil. ACR lists ductless mini-splits, PTACs, window units, and fan coil units as "cooling-heating units." We clean the coil and blower by hand. **No fogging on ductless units.** There is no duct to treat. Same logic as window units at ECU. See [PTAC and fan coil](/kb/jobs/systems/ptac-and-fan-coil/).

## What we touch and what we do not

| We clean | We do not touch |
|---|---|
| Supply and return ducts, plenums, registers, grilles | The outdoor condenser or heat pump coil. It is not in the airstream. |
| Evaporator (indoor) coil | Refrigerant lines, valves, or charge. That is a licensed HVAC tech. |
| Blower housing, wiped in place | Gas valve, burners, flue |
| Drain pan and line (look, photograph, clear if in scope) | Electrical and controls repairs |
| Filter: new one in before the system runs | |

**Report, do not fix:** rusted or cracked heat exchanger, a pan full of water, a tripped float switch, crushed or disconnected flex, a missing filter, a return that pulls from the crawlspace or attic.
