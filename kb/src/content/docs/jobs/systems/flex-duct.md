Insulated flexible duct: a wire-helix inner core with a plastic film liner, fiberglass insulation around it, and an outer jacket. Standard for residential branch runs and for the single return run up to an attic air handler. See [Attic air handler](/kb/jobs/systems/attic-air-handler/).

## The rules

1. **No access holes in flex.** ACR 2021 4.2.9: "Service openings shall not be made in flexible ductwork." You work it from its ends: the register boot at the room end, or the plenum or trunk takeoff at the other. The NADCA guide spec says to disconnect flex at both ends as needed and reconnect it to SMACNA standards.
1. **Easy on the whip.** The inner liner is thin film. Aggressive agitation tears it or collapses the run, and then it is a repair, not a cleaning.
1. **Reconnect it right.** If you pull a flex run off a collar, it goes back on to SMACNA standards (NADCA guide spec) and gets sealed. Tape and mastic closures for flex are covered by UL 181B.

## Clean it or replace it

The film liner inside flex counts as a **non-porous** surface under ACR, the same class as sheet metal. So flex in good shape gets cleaned like anything else.

Replace it when:
- **The inner core is torn or punctured.** A torn core opens the insulation to the airstream. Cleaning does not close it.
- **It is crushed, kinked flat, or collapsed** and will not hold its shape.
- **It cannot be brought to visibly clean.** ACR 2021 4.14.3 recommends replacing components that cannot reach the specified cleanliness through cleaning and restoration.

Replacement is not in our standard scope. It is a finding: photograph it, write it down, and tell the lead. See [What is in scope](/kb/jobs/rules/what-is-in-scope/) and [Inspection and photos](/kb/jobs/procedures/inspection-and-photos/).
