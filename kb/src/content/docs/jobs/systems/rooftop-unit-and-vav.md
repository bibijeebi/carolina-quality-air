Commercial. Rooftop air handler feeding a trunk above a drop ceiling, branch runs to ceiling diffusers, and **VAV boxes** on the supply side that modulate airflow per zone.

**What it means for the job**:
- Long hose runs from the roof or from a mechanical room. Suction drops over distance.
- The work happens above the ceiling. Pull tiles first, then cut.
- VAV boxes are real components you have to work around and sometimes open. A VAV box is a damper that opens and closes to meet the zone thermostat. Some have a reheat coil inside. One of the NADCA guide spec's sample scope lines is cleaning reheat coils and the supply duct downstream of them, so check whether the scope includes them. See [VAV boxes](/kb/jobs/systems/vav-boxes/).
- Trunk above the ceiling is usually **rectangular** sheet metal, wrapped in insulation. Pull the tile, score and roll the wrap, cut the 8 inch hole, square patch over it.
- **Dampers**: mark the position of any damper before cleaning and put it back where it was after. ACR 2021 4.8. That includes VAV dampers and balancing dampers. See [Dampers](/kb/jobs/systems/dampers/).
- **Fire dampers stay in service.** Do not remove one or wire it open to get a whip past. Work each side from its own access. _(CQA fire damper practice unconfirmed)_
- Occupied space means containment, timing, and noise management. Usually that means nights. See [Occupied buildings](/kb/jobs/rules/occupied-buildings/).

For how VAV compares with multizone, dual-duct, and constant volume systems, see [Duct system types](/kb/jobs/systems/duct-system-types/).
