## Who prices, and how the number moves

1. Jeff or Perry sets the price. Becky and Vicki transmit it. They do not set it.
1. A site walk means one of the two principals drives there. Every quote far from Greenville costs principal drive time.
1. A tech's job on pricing is to feed the principals: counts, photos, and what you saw inside the duct.

## Walk it

Jeff: "Honestly, pricing jobs isn't difficult. The main thing is, you have to have somebody go to it to walk around and look."

Blueprints and pictures alone do not paint the whole picture, and pricing without a walk leaves money on the table. His walk list:

- How tall are the ceilings?
- How far are the distances?
- How many vacuum hoses are we going to need?
- Where can we park?
- Where can we set up?

Every item on that list is hours. Ceiling height is ladder or lift time. Distance and hose count are setup time. Parking and staging are dead time. See [Walk the job](/kb/jobs/procedures/walk-the-job/).

## The Perry method

From a recorded estimate walk at a Winterville nonprofit, August 2026.

1. **Inspect short.** About three minutes: find the unit, name the system type, note access and where the smell is worst, get square footage from the customer.
1. **Teach long.** About fifteen minutes, until trust is built, then a soft close. The customer sells themselves.
1. **His set pieces:**
   - Freon is like the oil in your car. It cools and lubricates.
   - Fan on AUTO, not ON. ON blows moist air across a wet coil after it stops cooling, and mold grows. He has done hundreds of jobs since 1999 caused by that switch.
   - Closed doors cause short cycling, less dehumidifying, clammy air.
   - Filters: pleated, changed every three months. Never the see-through cheap ones. Cheap filter, dirty unit, dirty ducts, mold food.
1. **The teaching is the intake.** While he explains fan settings and filters he is learning service history, who pays (owner or tenant), and how neglected the system is.

**Cameras**: Perry's policy on that walk was that a camera is never part of an estimate. If the customer asks for it, the customer pays for it. _(The public site promises a camera look on residential inspections. Confirm with Jeff which rule applies where.)_

**Weekdays**: Perry steers customers to weekday scheduling and tells them why: weekend work costs more. Honest, and it protects the margin.

## Commercial cost stack

Perry, pricing a sub inquiry out loud:

| Line | Notes |
|---|---|
| Square footage base | The starting number. |
| Labor | Crew size times days. |
| Driving fee | A named line item. _(Set rate or judgment: unconfirmed.)_ |
| Overnight stay | About $150 per person per night for hotel and food. |
| Nights and weekends | Priced higher. |

- **Overnight work is where the money is.** Jeff: staying the night is where the jobs start adding up.
- **Capacity**: 4 to 5 crews, about 12 people, a 20,000 sq ft building in two days. Retail can start at Friday close and run through Sunday.
- **Do not confirm a number you did not build.** A sub asked Perry whether $4,000 per 3-man crew per day was right. He neither confirmed nor corrected it.
- **Two-day commercial benchmark**: Jeff described $20,000 to $25,000 as good money for a two-day commercial job. The Green Road brief proposed $24,000 on that basis. _(Final number: Jeff's call.)_

## Discounts are given loudly

On the estimate call, the $250 ozone comp and the $200 church discount were both itemized to the customer. The customer felt $450 of goodwill. A discount nobody hears about buys nothing.

Goodwill first can also mean the machine. Perry loaned the ozone machine the same day, overnight, free, to a customer whose building was closed from a smell. Relief before the job was booked. Ozone rule he gave: nobody inside while it runs, open both doors and wait 15 minutes before going in.

## Encapsulation: the upsell

- **What it is**: an antimicrobial coating sprayed over internally lined (fiberglass liner) duct **after** cleaning. Bare sheet metal just gets cleaned.
- **Spotting it**: bare metal showing on a supply trunk that should be lined means the liner is eroding. That is the flag.
- **Sequence**: you cannot encapsulate dirty duct. Clean first, always. It is an upsell on a current job, not a standalone. ACR agrees: coatings only after the system passes verification.
- **The line to the customer**: "There might be a chance this needs to be encapsulated. We won't know until we get into it."
- **The close**: take a lot of pictures. Show the customer. It is a lot easier to sell with photos, and a finished coat looks good, which sells the next one.
- **Material**: paint runs about $100 a gallon, roughly $500 a job.
- **Why it matters**: encapsulating usually bills more than the duct cleaning side of the same job. The alternative (rip out liner, wrap outside) goes to an HVAC contractor. Jeff wants more of it on commercial jobs: even one in five or one in ten adds a lot.

**Your part as a tech**: you are inside the duct every day. Bare metal on a lined supply trunk: photo it, text it to Jeff.

## Photos on every job

Take a lot of pictures on every job. The same set closes a mid-job upsell, lets Jeff re-price without a second trip, fills the completion file government jobs require, and markets the next job.
