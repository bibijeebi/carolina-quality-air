## Respirators and dust masks

| Situation | What the rule says |
|---|---|
| You wear an N95 by choice | Voluntary use. The employer gives you the Appendix D handout on respirators. |
| A respirator is required | Medical evaluation before fit test or use. |
| Tight-fitting facepiece | Fit tested before first use, on any new model or size, and at least once a year. |
| Beard | Facial hair between the seal and your face means no tight-fitting respirator. |

We carry N95s on jobs. Whether a job moves from voluntary to required depends on the hazard. Mold remediation, fire soot and anything suspected hazardous are where it changes. _(Company written respiratory program: unconfirmed. Ask Jeff.)_

## Ladders

- Inspect the ladder before first use in each shift. A ladder with a defect gets tagged "Dangerous: Do Not Use" and comes out of service.
- Use a ladder only for what it was designed for.
- To reach an upper landing (roof, mezzanine, attic floor), the side rails extend at least **3 feet** above the landing.
- Never set a ladder on boxes, buckets or other unstable bases.
- Do not tie two ladders together for length.

Most of our ladder time is drop ceilings. Move the ladder, do not lean off it.

## Fall protection

| Setting | Trigger height | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| General industry (29 CFR 1910.28) | 4 ft | Unprotected side or edge needs guardrail, net, or personal fall protection. |
| Construction (29 CFR 1926.501) | 6 ft | Same, for construction work. |

Rooftop units are the common fall exposure. So are open attic joists, where the fall is through the ceiling.

## Lifts

| Machine | What OSHA calls it | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Scissor lift | A mobile scaffold, not an aerial lift | Guardrails are the fall protection. Stay on the platform floor. |
| Boom lift, vertical mast lift | Aerial lift (MEWP in the ANSI standards) | Only authorized persons operate. Test controls each day before use. Stand on the basket floor. Never sit or climb on the rail. Tie off to the basket, never to a nearby structure. |

High commercial ceilings mean a rental lift. We have run a Sunbelt-rented vertical mast lift on a commercial drop-ceiling job, and the Green Road bid carries a lift rental. The ANSI A92.22 and A92.24 standards require MEWP operator training, and CQA has sent techs through a Sunbelt lift course. On a GC's site, their safety officer decides who runs the lift.

## Forklifts

Forklifts are powered industrial trucks under 29 CFR 1910.178.

1. **Training has three parts**: formal instruction (online or classroom), practical training, and an evaluation of you operating in the workplace.
1. **The evaluation repeats at least every three years**, and sooner after an incident, a near miss, or a switch to a different type of truck.
1. **An online card is only the first part.** You are not certified to drive until someone evaluates you on the truck.

On sub jobs the forklift usually belongs to the client. Do not drive it unless the site's safety officer authorizes you. Drill: [Forklift drill](/learning/forklift-drill.html).

## Heat

There is no OSHA or NC heat standard. NC DOL can cite heat hazards under the General Duty Clause. Their guidance:

- Drink water regularly.
- Take breaks in shade or cool areas during heavy work in heat and humidity.
- Schedule the hardest work for the cooler part of the day.
- Wear light, loose, breathable clothing. PPE raises heat risk.

Our practice in attics and closed mechanical rooms: fans, water, ice, rotate people out. Dizzy means get out, hydrate, and tell the lead.

## Confined spaces

A confined space is big enough to bodily enter, has limited ways in and out, and is not meant for continuous occupancy. It becomes a **permit-required** space if it has, or could have, a hazardous atmosphere, engulfment, a layout that can trap you, or another serious hazard.

Crawlspaces, large plenums, and walk-in air handlers can meet the first definition. If the space smells of gas or sewage, has standing water, or you could get wedged, stop and call the lead before anyone goes in. _(Whether a given CQA site counts as a permit space is a site call, not a blanket rule.)_

## Electrical

- The portable vacuums run on 20 amp plugs. Adapter plugs are a one-of-a-kind item, so check which van has them.
- Power off before you pull an air handler panel.
- Electric heat strips get locked out and tagged out before cleaning. That is an ACR requirement, not a suggestion.
- Keep cords, plugs and ground fault protection in good repair. ACR lists them as equipment the contractor must maintain.
- Never spray water or cleaner on electrical parts. ACR bans it.

## Chemicals and fogging

- The Safety Data Sheet for every chemical on the job stays on site for the whole job. ACR requires it.
- Use every product exactly per its label. See [Chemicals](/kb/chemicals/bio-fresh-cd/).
- **Put smoke detectors in test mode before fogging.** The fogger sets them off. ACR also requires a plan when duct smoke detectors go offline.
- **Ozone**: nobody inside while it runs. The first person back opens both doors and waits 15 minutes before going in. See [Ozone machine](/kb/gear/air-treatment/ozone/).

## Asbestos and old buildings

Pre-1980s buildings can have asbestos in duct wrap, mastic and pipe insulation. Vermiculite is treated as asbestos until tested. Do not cut, scrape or whip it. Stop and tell the lead.
