Work comes in four ways: a homeowner or business calls, a restoration company subs us a scope, a mechanical contractor carries our number in a public bid, or we bid a government job directly. Each one expects something different on the day.

## Homeowners

- Call in, a principal or the office sets the visit. Jeff or Perry prices it.
- They are afraid of one thing: getting scammed. The "$99 whole house" coupon outfits did that. Be the opposite: show them what came out, patch properly, take photos.
- Single-system homes run 3 to 5 hours. Plastic down, check the wheels, put every register back straight.
- They get a NADCA certificate at the end.

## Churches and nonprofits

Perry's walk at a Winterville nonprofit is the model: three minutes of inspection and fifteen of teaching (fan on AUTO, not ON; pleated filters every three months). The church discount is standard practice and it is said out loud. One good job inside a church network brings the next several. See [Pricing](/kb/company/pricing/).

## Commercial: retail and restaurants

- Open businesses cannot have tube across the sales floor, so it is nights (TJ Maxx ran 9 PM to about 5 AM) or weekends.
- Perry steers customers to weekdays where he can, and tells them why: weekend work costs more.
- Expect a manager to let you in and lock up. Leave it cleaner than you found it.

## Schools and universities

- Summer is the window. Dorms are room by room, fan coils and PTACs.
- At ECU Clement Hall, Jeff had the AC units surveyed floor by floor and reported which were broken or noisy. That list is a service lead for the customer. Whoever does the survey generates the lead.

## Government and institutional

- **How it reaches us**: public bids post on the NC eVP portal (evp.nc.gov). Some are direct invitations for bid, lowest responsive price wins. Some are term contracts. Some list duct cleaning as one line inside a larger HVAC renovation.
- **What the spec usually says**: clean per NADCA ACR, before-and-after photos, sometimes encapsulation. ASCS on staff matters here.
- **On site**: security clearance or badge in, escorts, tool counts in and out, restricted work windows. Jails and prisons pack returns with compressed lint. Everything takes longer than it looks, and the price has to carry that.
- **Paperwork**: photos of every section, before and after. Government completion files want them.

### Bid shapes we have seen

| Shape | Example | How it prices |
|---|---|---|
| Direct bid, one-time | NC Dept. of Adult Correction, Johnston CI dorm duct cleaning and lining, 2025 | Price per building, three references, signed forms. No bond. Lowest price wins. |
| Term contract | Wake County Public Schools duct cleaning, 2026 to 2027 plus options | One all-in hourly labor rate (equipment, mileage, overhead included), overtime at 1.5x, materials and disposal at cost plus 10%, 1-hour emergency response. |
| Line item in a prime's bid | City of Raleigh, Green Road Community Center HVAC renovation, 2026 | We do not bid the city. We quote the duct cleaning line to every mechanical contractor bidding. Whoever wins, we work. |
| Sub to a vendor | UNC Wilmington campus laundry contract (dryer vents yearly, May to July) | The laundry vendor must clean every dryer line yearly and does not do vents. We would be their sub. _(Lead, not yet work.)_ |

Lead reports and bid documents: [/leads/](/leads/). The Green Road pre-bid brief: [/estimates/green-road.html](/estimates/green-road.html).

What a bid response needs from the company, once: state vendor registration with the HVAC services commodity code, local vendor registrations where required, a one-page capability sheet, three written references, and a current insurance certificate.

## Restoration companies

- Restoration crews do demo and dry-out and sub out specialty scopes. HVAC cleaning is one of them. Jeff: we get a lot of work from Servpro and Sasser.
- **Each franchise is its own buyer.** Servpro Greenville and Servpro North Raleigh are two relationships.
- **The pitch is capacity**, not quality: we travel (Richmond, DC), we can field 4 to 5 crews and do 20,000 sq ft in two days, and we answer the phone.
- **On their jobs, their rules.** At Worth Higgins in Richmond, Servpro was the GC: their start, end and lunch times, their timesheet, their safety officer, and their containment (sheeting with zip or hook-and-loop doors). We still seal the system ourselves.

## Mechanical contractors

On a renovation, the mechanical contractor replaces equipment and the existing duct gets cleaned and reconnected. That cleaning line has to be carried by somebody. Our quote to them should:

- Name NADCA ACR as the method.
- State crew size, days on site, and mobilization lead time, so our part of their schedule is on paper.
- Carry optional lines (coil cleaning, alternates) separately so every prime sees them.
- Exclude equipment, controls, testing and balancing, and duct that is being thrown away.

## What every customer gets

- A crew that looks before it cuts.
- Plastic down and registers back on straight.
- Photos.
- Access holes closed to the standard. See [Standards](/kb/company/standards/).
- A straight answer when something is beyond a cleaning. Mold and failing liner get named, photographed, and sent to Jeff to price.
