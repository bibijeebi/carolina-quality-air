Read this before your first job. Then read [The job, generically](/kb/jobs/the-job/). That is the whole day in nine phases.

## How you get on a job

1. **Jeff posts the job in the crew group text.** First to volunteer gets the slot.
1. **The text gives you the site and a start time.** If it says "probably 9", confirm the time the night before. A two hour drive on a guessed time is a wasted morning.
1. **Meet point** is Jeff's house or the site itself. The text says which. Van assignments get sorted at the meet point.
1. **Be there 15 minutes early.** Parked, dressed, ready.

Scheduling for the week comes from the office by email (Vicki). Hours go to payroll (Meredith).

Multi-day out-of-town jobs: Jeff arranges a hotel. If you live within driving distance of the site you can drive home each night instead.

## After each job day

Text Meredith directly. Jeff asks new people to do this from day one. Include:

1. Your full name.
1. "Worked with Carolina Quality Air at" the site.
1. The date or dates.
1. Start and end time.

On out-of-town jobs Jeff's instruction is to include drive time. Ask Jeff if you are unsure which legs count.

## What to wear

| Item | Why |
|---|---|
| Long sleeves | Fiberglass insulation and sheet metal edges. Short sleeves means itching and cuts. |
| Work pants | Work pants or straight-fit work jeans you can kneel and crawl in. Khakis wear through fast under houses and in attics. Slim fits bind when crawling. |
| Closed-toe sneakers or tennis shoes | Jeff's call for most jobs. You walk all day on commercial work, and a hustler runs a little. Work boots are not needed. |
| Pull-on rubber boots | For wet work and crawlspaces. |
| The site shirt | On sub jobs the prime contractor hands out shirts on site, as at TJ Maxx and the Durham courthouse. They are the prime's shirts, not ours. Some primes collect them back at the end. Give it back when asked. |

Knee pads if you have them. There is a lot of kneeling.

## What to bring

- Water, and more of it in summer. Attics are hot.
- Lunch or snacks. The food stop happens on the way, not on site. On long days Jeff often calls in or picks up a group order. Text him your order as a plain item list.
- Phone and charger. Photos are part of the job.
- Work gloves.
- Utility knife.
- A screwdriver or multi-bit driver. Registers come off with screws.
- Dust masks (N95) and safety glasses.
- Photo ID. Jails, courthouses and some schools badge you in.

## Before the van leaves

**Check what is in your van.** The most common way a day goes wrong is a specialty tool riding in the wrong van. Adapter plugs, hole cutters, the soaker hose, the Cool Jet. If the job needs it and only one exists, find out which van has it before anyone drives off. Details: [Roll out](/kb/jobs/procedures/roll-out/).

## How the day goes

1. Roll out and food stop.
1. Walk the job with the lead. Find the unit, figure out supply and return, find the hazards.
1. Stage and protect. Plastic down, registers pulled and stacked in one place.
1. Set the negative air.
1. Cut access.
1. Clean. Branches before the trunk, working back toward the negative air. Negative air runs the whole time you agitate.
1. Clean the air handler and coil.
1. Patch every hole, put it all back, walk it with the customer.

You will probably start on register pull and stack, or as runner. See [Crew and roles](/kb/reference/crew-and-roles/).

## House rules

- **Phone away** unless you are taking job photos.
- **Ask.** Nobody expects you to know what a VAV box is on day one. They do expect you to ask before you cut.
- **Take pictures.** Before, during, after. Photos close upsells and they are the paperwork government jobs require.
- **Prices come from Jeff and Perry.** If a customer asks what something costs, say Jeff or Perry will get them a number. Do not guess one.
- **Check the wheels** before you roll a vacuum across a customer floor. Paint from the last job tracks.
- **On a GC's job, their rules apply.** When we sub for a restoration company, their safety officer runs the site. Do not drive the client's forklift or lift without their say-so.
- **Stop work** for anything on the [stop-work list](/kb/jobs/rules/hazards-and-stop-work/). Asbestos-looking material, visible mold, animal infestation. Tell the lead.

## Learn these words first

Twelve words carry most of the talk on a job. Full list in the [Glossary](/kb/reference/glossary/).

| Word | Means |
|---|---|
| Supply | Ducts that push conditioned air into rooms. |
| Return | Ducts that pull room air back to the unit. |
| Air handler (AHU) | The box with the blower and coil. |
| Plenum | The box on the air handler where the duct connects. |
| Trunk | The main duct off the plenum. |
| Register / grille | The cover on the opening in the room. Strictly, a register has a damper and a grille does not. On the job everyone says register. |
| Negative air | The machine that pulls, and the suction it puts on the system. |
| Whip | The agitation tool on the airline. |
| Forward | The nozzle that pushes debris toward the suction. |
| Pop plug | Closes a 1 inch bit hole. |
| Patch | Sheet metal square that closes an 8 inch access. |
| FSK tape | The foil tape that seals insulation back over a patch. |

:::tip[Memory hook]
**Supply pushes, return pulls. Branches, then trunk, then the handler last.** Details in [Order of operations](/kb/jobs/rules/order-of-operations/).
:::

## Study

[DuctStudy](/learning/) has drills for the NADCA ASCS exam and the forklift card. The Shop Book pages are where the drills come from.

Jeff wants crew to get ASCS certified. He keeps NADCA study books, including the HVAC Inspection Manual, and lends them out. Ask him before you buy study material. Exam format and where to sit it: [Standards](/kb/company/standards/#nadca-certifications).

Spanish is a plus on the crew. Some site crews are Spanish-speaking, and a tech who can give and take job instructions in Spanish is a real help on site.
