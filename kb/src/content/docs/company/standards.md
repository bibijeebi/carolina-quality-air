## ACR in the field

These are the "shall" items from the 2021 edition that change how you work. Section numbers in brackets.

### Before cleaning

- **Inspect before and after** every cleaning job. [1.1]
- **Written work plan** before work starts: scope, engineering controls, products. [2.0, 2.2]
- **SDS for every chemical** in the work plan and kept on site for the whole job. [2.7.1]
- **Life safety devices.** If duct smoke detectors have to go offline, the plan covers it. [2.8]
- **Equipment comes in clean.** Everything is cleaned and inspected before it enters the site, and cleaned or sealed before it moves between areas or leaves. [3.1.1, 3.1.3]

### Negative pressure and containment

- **Continuous negative pressure** on the section being cleaned, relative to the occupied space. Verify it during the job. [3.4, 4.1, 4.1.1]
- **Exhaust indoors means HEPA.** A collector exhausting inside the building must be HEPA filtered, 99.97% at 0.3 micron. Non-HEPA collectors exhaust outdoors. [3.3, 4.1.2, 4.1.3]
- **Level 1 containment** is the minimum on every job. [3.9]
- **Contaminated debris is contained** before it leaves the building. [3.5]
- Redundant equipment on site in case negative pressure fails is recommended. [3.8]
- **The vacuum runs the whole time** you are cleaning. [4.5]

### Cleaning

- **Agitate, then capture.** Dislodge with an agitation device, collect with a vacuum collection device. [4.3, 4.7.2, 4.7.3]
- **Mark dampers** before cleaning and put them back in the marked position. [4.8]
- **No water or cleaner on electrical, fibrous glass, or porous components.** [4.4]
- **Lined duct and duct board**: the method must not abrade, break or tear the liner. [4.17.1]
- **Coils**: dry clean first. Wet clean if dry does not get it, or if there is suspect microbial growth. Clean and flush the drain pan and test that it drains. Electric heat strips get locked out and tagged out. [4.11]

### Service openings (access holes)

- Openings must not hurt the duct's structure, insulation or airflow, and must close properly. [4.2.1 to 4.2.3]
- **Patch panels** are the same gauge or heavier, fastened with screws or rivets **at least every 4 inches on center**, overlapping the duct **at least 1 inch on all sides**. [4.2.5.1, 4.2.5.2]
- Tapes and mastics used to close openings meet **UL 181A/B**. [4.2.5.4]
- In fibrous glass, no exposed glass edges in the airstream, and removed insulation is replaced with like material of the same thickness. [4.2.8]
- **No service openings in flex duct.** [4.2.9]

How we do it: [Cut an 8 inch access](/kb/jobs/procedures/cut-an-8-inch-access/), [Patch a sheet metal access](/kb/jobs/procedures/patch-a-sheet-metal-access/), [Pop plug a bit hole](/kb/jobs/procedures/pop-plug-a-bit-hole/).

### Chemicals and coatings

- Every cleaning agent or chemical is used exactly per the manufacturer's instructions. [3.7]
- Products comply with local, state and federal rules. [4.12]
- **Surface treatments (coatings) go on only after the system is cleaned and passes verification.** They must meet UL 181. [4.15, 4.15.1] This is why we say you cannot encapsulate dirty duct.
- Mold remediation follows ANSI/IICRC S520. [4.13]

### Cleanliness verification

| Method | Used on | Pass |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Visual inspection | Everything | Visibly clean: free of non-adhered substances and debris. If it passes, no further test is needed. |
| 2. Surface Comparison Test | Porous surfaces (liner, duct board) | HEPA contact vacuum with a 2.5 in nylon brush, 4 passes. Clean if no significant visible difference. |
| 2. NADCA Vacuum Test | Non-porous surfaces (sheet metal) | Template, cassette and pump, lab weighed. Clean at **0.75 mg/100 cm²** or less. |

Verification happens right after cleaning and before the system runs. [5.1] Method 2 is for when the visual result is inconclusive or disputed. [5.2.1] Photos and records documenting the work are recommended. [5.6]

### Table 1: inspection intervals

| Building | Air handler | Supply duct | Return / exhaust duct |
|---|---|---|---|
| Residential | 1 year | 2 years | 2 years |
| Commercial | 1 year | 1 year | 1 year |
| Industrial | 1 year | 1 year | 1 year |
| Healthcare | 1 year | 1 year | 1 year |
| Marine | 1 year | 2 years | 2 years |

These are **inspection** intervals, not cleaning intervals.

:::tip[Memory hook]
**Only houses and boats get two.** Everything else is one year across the board. The air handler is one year everywhere.
:::

## NADCA certifications

| Cert | What it is | Prerequisite | Renewal |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ASCS**, Air Systems Cleaning Specialist | The core cleaning certification. Written exam. | None | Every year by June 30, with 6 CECs earned July 1 to June 30. |
| **CVI**, Certified Ventilation Inspector | Inspection of HVAC systems. | Active ASCS | Every 3 years by June 30. |
| **VSMR**, Ventilation System Mold Remediator | Mold remediation in ventilation systems. Launched 2003. | Comes with ASCS. NADCA's ASCS Candidate's Guide: "The ASCS certificate holder also qualifies as a Ventilation System Mold Remediator (VSMR)." | With ASCS. |

- The ASCS exam is $385 at the member rate at a testing center, $100 to retake. The 2026 Fall Technical Conference listed the same $385 member price for the ASCS exam. Register through nadcatesting.com with the company's Member Security Code.
- The ASCS exam is 150 multiple-choice questions in 3.5 hours.
- Where to sit it: a Kryterion testing center, or a NADCA conference. The nearest testing center on the Raleigh side is NC Elite Career Service Center, 132 Quade Dr, Cary. Other NC centers: BCY Testing Solution in Greensboro, Craven Community College Testing Center in New Bern, NC Elite in Wilmington.
- Jeff's plan is for techs to sit the ASCS at NADCA's Fall Technical Conference. When the exam is taken there, registration goes through the conference and the office handles it. The 2026 Fall Technical Conference ran September 10 to 12, 2026, in Norfolk, VA. The 2027 one is September 17 to 19, 2027, in Concord, NC.

ASCS exam domains, by weight:

| Domain | Weight |
|---|---|
| I. Necessity for Cleaning | 5% |
| II. Mechanical Systems | 15% |
| III. HVAC System Inspections | 12% |
| IV. Contamination | 12% |
| V. Cleaning and Restoration Procedures | 42% |
| VI. Health and Safety | 6% |
| VII. Standards and Guidelines | 8% |

Domain V is nearly half the test. Study the cleaning, access, engineering-control and verification parts of ACR hardest.

Study material: ACR, The NADCA Standard, is a free PDF from NADCA. Jeff keeps NADCA study books, including the HVAC Inspection Manual, and lends them to crew going for ASCS. Ask him before you buy anything.
- NADCA recommends that at least one owner, principal or key full-time employee who manages projects holds ASCS.
- ACR names ASCS and CVI as the kind of qualified inspector to use for the pre-cleaning assessment. [1.2]

## Dryer vents

**Certification**: CSIA (Chimney Safety Institute of America) issues the **Certified Dryer Exhaust Technician (C-DET)**. CSIA calls it the only nationally recognized credential for dryer vent cleaning. Nobody at CQA holds it _(confirm)_.

**Code**: the 2021 International Residential Code, section M1502, is the model code for residential dryer exhaust. North Carolina adopts its own edition of the residential code, so check the NC edition before quoting a code section to a customer.

| M1502 item | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Independent system | Dryer exhaust connects to nothing else and goes outdoors. |
| Termination | Outside the building, with a backdraft damper. **No screens.** At least 3 ft from openings if the dryer maker does not say otherwise. |
| Duct | Smooth interior metal, at least 0.0157 in (28 gauge), 4 in nominal. |
| Joints | Sealed and mechanically fastened. No screws poking more than 1/8 in into the duct. |
| Transition duct | One length, listed to UL 2158A, 8 ft max, never concealed in a wall. |
| Booster fans | Domestic booster fans are prohibited. |
| Length | 35 ft max from the transition connection to the outlet, minus fitting allowances (a mitered 90 costs 5 ft). Longer runs need a label within 6 ft of the connection. |

What a screen on the hood, a crushed transition hose, or a vinyl transition means for the customer: it is a code problem, not only a cleaning problem. Show them the photo.
