Encapsulation is spraying a coating over the inside of **internally lined** (fiberglass liner) duct **after** it has been cleaned, to resurface liner that is wearing out. It is the commercial upsell Jeff wants more of. It is also the service the standards are most careful about, so it gets sold for the right reason: failing liner, not as a routine add-on.

<dl class="facts">
<dt>Goes on</dt><dd>Cleaned, dry, well-bonded fiberglass liner that is friable, abraded or eroding</dd>
<dt>Never on</dt><dd>Dirty duct. Wet liner. Liner with mold growth. Liner that is delaminating.</dd>
<dt>Standard</dt><dd>ACR 4.15 (surface treatments, meet UL 181), 4.18 (resurfacing fiberglass), 4.19 (replace when it cannot be restored)</dd>

## Coat or replace

| Liner condition | Call | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Dry, well-bonded, light surface fuzz after cleaning | Leave it | Cleaned liner in good shape does not need a coating. |
| Dry, friable or eroding, **bare metal showing on the supply side** | **Coat** after cleaning | ACR 4.18: resurfacing is allowed where liner shows friability, abrasion or degradation, if it gives a strong, bondable surface after proper mechanical cleaning. |
| Will not hold a coating (loose, flaking facing, delaminating) | Replace | ACR 4.18.2: if the material cannot support the coating or hold it long term, do not resurface. 4.19: replacement is recommended. |
| Wet, or has been wet | Replace | NAIMA via NADCA: fiberglass wet in service should be removed and replaced. EPA: wet or moldy insulation cannot be effectively cleaned. |
| Mold growth on the liner | Replace. Stop and call Jeff. | NADCA: porous materials with mold growth are removed and replaced. See [Mold and IAQ](/kb/services/mold-and-iaq/). |
| Old coating peeling into the airstream | Remove the failed coating | Coating over a failed coat repeats the failure. _(field practice)_ |

Replacement (tear out liner, wrap the outside) goes to an HVAC contractor and costs the customer much more. Encapsulating in place keeps the job in house. That is only a good trade when the liner can take it.

## What the standards say

- **NADCA**: "In no case should a coating be used in lieu of source-removal cleaning." Surfaces to be coated are thoroughly cleaned and evaluated first. Codes or specs may require coatings tested to ASTM E84 and ASTM C411.
- **ACR 4.15**: surface treatments go on only after the system is cleaned and passes verification, and must meet UL 181.
- **EPA**: most organizations concerned with duct cleaning, EPA included, do not recommend the **routine** use of sealants to encapsulate contaminants. And no product is EPA-registered as a biocide for fiberglass duct board or lined duct.

That last point matters for our paint. If it is sold as antimicrobial, its label decides what surfaces it can go on. Read the label before it goes on fiberglass liner. If the label does not list lined duct, tell the lead.

## How CQA sells it

1. On any lined duct job, look for **bare metal on the supply side**. That is liner eroding off.
1. Photograph it. Text the photos to Jeff mid-job.
1. The line to the customer: "There might be a chance this needs to be encapsulated. We won't know until we get into it."
1. Encapsulation is its own line on the ticket, after the cleaning.

## How CQA does it

From [JOB-026](/kb/jobs/write-ups/job-026-ronald-mcdonald-house-greenville/):

1. **Clean first. Always.** A coating over dirt seals the dirt in. See [Whip and forward a run](/kb/jobs/procedures/whip-and-forward-a-run/).
1. Photograph the liner, bare spots included.
1. Spray the coating with the airless rig, per the product label. Keep overspray off dampers, VAV sensors and anything that moves.
1. Photograph after, same angles.
1. **Clean the sprayer right away.** It clogs. It came back paint-caked once and the gun may need a rebuild kit. Pressure-test its hoses before the job. See [hoses](/kb/gear/air/hoses/#other-hoses-on-the-trucks).

The before-and-after set from Ronald McDonald House is the sales tool for the next one.

## Not the same thing

- **Clean and coat** is coil work: coil cleaning then a protective coil coating, usually on the coast. See [JOB-009](/kb/jobs/write-ups/job-009-beach-clean-and-coat/) and [Coil and unit cleaning](/kb/services/coil-and-unit-cleaning/).
- **Sealing duct leaks** with mastic at joints and patches is closure work, not encapsulation. See [Patch a sheet metal access](/kb/jobs/procedures/patch-a-sheet-metal-access/).
