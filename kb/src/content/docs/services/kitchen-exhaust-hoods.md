Kitchen exhaust cleaning is hood, filters, plenum, grease duct and rooftop fan, cleaned on a fire code schedule. **It is not part of a duct cleaning.** It is its own trade, its own code and its own chemistry, and on a standard job it is [out of scope](/kb/jobs/rules/what-is-in-scope/). CQA has done it once, at [Logan's Roadhouse](/kb/jobs/write-ups/job-022-logans-roadhouse-greenville/). Making it a regular service line is a proposal, not a decision. See [Proposal № 007, the hood lane](/proposals/2026-09-04-hood-lane.html).

<dl class="facts">
<dt>Code</dt><dd>NFPA 96, Ventilation Control and Fire Protection of Commercial Cooking Operations</dd>
<dt>Method standard</dt><dd>ANSI/IKECA C10 (cleaning). I10 covers inspection, M10 maintenance between cleanings.</dd>
<dt>Credential</dt><dd>IKECA CECT (technician) and CECS (specialist)</dd>
<dt>Target</dt><dd>Remove combustible grease deposits. The trade calls it "bare metal."</dd>
<dt>CQA status</dt><dd>One job done. Service line proposed.</dd>
</dl>

## Why it is a different trade

- **The contaminant is fuel.** Grease in a kitchen duct is what burns in a restaurant fire. Dust in a supply duct is not.
- **The code is a fire code.** NADCA ACR covers general, bathroom and heat recovery exhaust. Kitchen grease exhaust falls under NFPA 96, with cleaning to IKECA C10.
- **The method is wet.** Degreaser and hot water, not negative air and a whip. Runoff has to be caught and disposed of properly, never sent to a storm drain.
- **The paperwork goes to the fire marshal.** Service labels and a report, not a NADCA certificate.

## What NFPA 96 asks for

| Item | What it says |
|---|---|
| Inspection schedule (Table 11.4) | Monthly for solid fuel (wood, charcoal). Quarterly for high volume (24-hour, charbroiling, wok). Semiannual for moderate volume. Annual for low volume (churches, day camps, seasonal). |
| Who cleans (11.6.1) | If the system is contaminated with grease deposits, it is cleaned by a properly trained, qualified and certified company or person acceptable to the authority having jurisdiction. |
| How clean (11.6.2) | Hoods, grease removal devices, fans, ducts and other appurtenances are cleaned to remove combustible contaminants before surfaces become heavily contaminated with grease or oily sludge. |
| After cleaning (11.6.8) | The system is not coated with powder or any other substance. |
| Labels (11.6.9 to 11.6.12) | Where an access panel is opened, a label or tag with the company name and date goes near it. |

Section numbers are from the 2011 edition as cited by state inspectors. Later editions may number them differently. Check the edition the local fire marshal enforces. _(NC edition unconfirmed)_

## What Logan's taught

[JOB-022](/kb/jobs/write-ups/job-022-logans-roadhouse-greenville/), June 2026. A restaurant after a fire, kitchen exhaust never cleaned.

- A never-cleaned system after a fire is the worst case the trade has. It took everything the crew had.
- A system on a schedule is a fraction of that effort. The second visit is always easier than the first. That is the whole argument for maintained accounts.
- Summer heat in a closed kitchen is its own hazard. Water, shade breaks, a fan.
- Soot and grease together need a stronger cleaner than grille wash. See [Fire and soot](/kb/services/fire-and-soot-restoration/).

## The credential

IKECA's CECS exam (January 2026 handbook): 100 multiple-choice questions, 2 hours, closed book, 70% to pass, at a test center or by live remote proctor. Renews every June 1 with 1.5 CEUs a year. No state license is required to clean hoods in NC per the proposal. _(confirm with the local fire marshal)_

## If you get sent to a hood job

1. Kitchen cold and closed. Confirm cooking equipment is off before you start.
1. Plastic over the cookline and floor. Sleeve the hood so runoff goes to a catch, not the floor.
1. Degreaser on, dwell, hot water from the fan down.
1. Scrape what water will not move. Wet-vac the runoff.
1. Photograph every section before and after. Label every access panel you opened.
1. Grease runoff does not go down a storm drain.

Everything on this list comes from the proposal and IKECA practice, not from a CQA procedure that has been run and written up. _(unconfirmed)_
