CQA cleans HVAC systems. Mold remediation is a different service under a different standard. When a system shows suspect growth, the job changes from cleaning to a finding. This page is where that line sits.

<dl class="facts">
<dt>On the job</dt><dd>Visible growth is on the <a href="/kb/jobs/rules/hazards-and-stop-work/">stop-work list</a>. Stop, photograph, tell the lead.</dd>
<dt>Say</dt><dd>"Suspect microbial growth." Never "mold" without a lab result.</dd>
<dt>Standard</dt><dd>ACR 4.13: remediation follows ANSI/IICRC S520 plus ACR's component rules</dd>
<dt>NC licensing</dt><dd>None. No federal or state certification exists for mold remediation.</dd>
</dl>

## What we do

1. **Stop disturbing it.** Do not whip, blow or vacuum across it.
1. **Photograph it** where it is, with something for scale.
1. **Tell the lead, who tells Jeff.** Jeff decides the scope and the price.
1. **Tell the customer what we see, not what it is.** "Suspect microbial growth. A lab can confirm it."
1. **Recommend a tape-lift sample.** EPA: for about $50, some labs can tell whether a sample on clear household tape is mold.
1. **Recommend the system stay off.** EPA: do not run an HVAC system you know or suspect is contaminated with mold.
1. **Find the water.** Growth needs moisture. EPA: if the cause is not corrected, growth will come back.

## What we do not do

- Call a stain mold.
- Advertise or promise mold remediation, or claim a state license. NC has none to claim.
- Promise health results. Cleaning removes contamination from the system. It does not cure allergies or asthma.
- Fog to "kill mold." The fogger puts deodorizer in a clean system. See [Fog the system](/kb/jobs/procedures/fog-the-system/).
- Use ozone as a mold treatment. See [Ozone machine](/kb/gear/air-treatment/ozone/).
- Coat over it. Encapsulation goes on clean, dry, sound liner only. See [Encapsulation and coatings](/kb/services/encapsulation-and-coatings/).
- Use a product inside the system whose label does not say HVAC. No biocide is EPA-registered for fiberglass duct board or lined duct.

## Where it grows

| Spot | Why |
|---|---|
| Evaporator coil and drain pan | Wet every cooling cycle. A pan that does not drain holds water. |
| Anywhere after a leak or flood | Wet porous material. |

Perry's teaching on the causes, from customer walks: fan on **AUTO**, not ON, so the fan does not keep blowing moist air across a wet coil after cooling stops. Pleated filters changed every three months. See [JOB-027](/kb/jobs/write-ups/job-027-koinonia-estimate-walk/).

## Porous vs hard surfaces

| Surface | With suspect growth |
|---|---|
| Sheet metal, coil, pan | Can be cleaned. EPA lists substantial visible mold inside hard-surface ducts as a reason to clean. |
| Fiberglass liner, duct board, flex core | Replace. EPA: wet or moldy insulation cannot be effectively cleaned. NADCA: porous materials with mold growth are removed and replaced. |

## When it is bigger than us

EPA's commercial building guide scales containment to the area: limited containment for 10 to 100 sq ft, full containment over 100 sq ft. If sampling is needed or the area is large, the owner needs an indoor environmental professional to assess it. NADCA's VSMR credential covers mold remediation in ventilation systems. Per NADCA's ASCS Candidate's Guide, an ASCS holder also qualifies as VSMR, so every ASCS on our crew counts. See [Standards](/kb/company/standards/).

## IAQ claims

The company rules for anything said to a customer or put on the website: no health claims, no energy-savings numbers, no "kills 99.9%," no routine yearly duct cleaning. What we can say: what came out of their system, with before-and-after photos.
