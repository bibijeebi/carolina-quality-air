## The Triangle has two duct problems, not one

**New construction.** Holly Springs, Apex, Fuquay-Varina, Wake Forest,
Rolesville and Clayton have been building continuously for a decade. A new
house is a tight, well-sealed envelope with the air handler and flex duct sat
in a vented attic. Two things follow from that. First, the system spends its
first year circulating drywall dust, sawdust and construction debris that got
into the ducts before the registers went on. Second, a tight envelope means
whatever is in the ductwork stays in the house — there is far less accidental
ventilation than an older home has.

New homeowners are often surprised that a two-year-old house needs anything.
The build debris is the reason.

**Everything built before about 2000.** Inside-the-beltline Raleigh, older
Cary neighbourhoods like Preston, MacGregor Downs and Lochmere, and much of
Chapel Hill sit on crawlspaces with metal trunk lines and fibreglass duct
board that has been shedding for twenty years. Different problem, different
approach, same inspection to figure out which you have.

## Pollen is the thing people here actually notice

The Triangle's pollen season is genuinely extreme. NC's environmental agency
recorded a record tree-pollen count in Raleigh of 16,284 grains per cubic
metre on the first of April, and the visible yellow pine pollen peaks from
late March through mid-April. Tree season as a whole runs from February into
May.

None of that stops at your front door. It comes in through the return, settles
in the ductwork, and gets stirred back up every time the system cycles. It is
the most common reason Triangle customers call us for the first time.
