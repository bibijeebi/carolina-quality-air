## Coastal systems age differently

Salt air is not a marketing line down here, it is a measurable difference.
Outdoor equipment in coastal zones corrodes far faster than the same unit ten
miles inland — condenser coils pit and perforate, and a system that should
give you fifteen to twenty years can be finished in eight to ten without
protection. It is worse the closer you are to the water: Wrightsville,
Carolina Beach, Kure Beach and up the Topsail strand.

Inside the house, the same salt aerosol works on registers, supply boots and
sheet-metal trunk lines. We see rust in Wilmington ductwork that simply does
not show up in Greenville.

Add the highest humidity load in the state and air conditioning equipment
that is frequently oversized for the house, and you get short cycling, indoor
humidity that never really comes down, and microbial growth right at the
supply boots — often somewhere the homeowner can see it, which is usually the
call we get.

## Three kinds of house, three kinds of problem

Wilmington's housing stock is unusually split.

**Downtown historic.** The National Register district covers more than 230
blocks, with contributing buildings from the 1700s and 1800s — one of the
largest historic districts in the South. That means dirt or partial
crawlspaces and ductwork retrofitted into buildings never designed to have
any. These jobs take longer and we quote them after looking, never before.

**The suburban ring.** Ogden, Porters Neck, and the Monkey Junction area:
mostly 1980s–2000s, conventional crawlspace or slab, and fairly predictable.

**The new builds.** Leland, Hampstead, Porters Neck and out into Brunswick —
slab-on-grade with the air handler in the attic. Same construction-debris
story as the Triangle, with more humidity behind it.

## Houses that took water in Florence

Florence dropped 23 inches of rain on Wilmington in four days in September
2018, with a record storm surge on the Cape Fear, and every road into New
Hanover County closed. Homes that flooded then can still hold wet cavity
insulation and contaminated ductwork years later.

We will tell you plainly what we find. If a system needs remediation rather
than cleaning, that is what the report will say — cleaning a duct that has a
moisture source still feeding it is a waste of your money.
