how to pass

How this works. 436 questions across all five CVI domains. Hit Study and answer. Right on the first try and it retires. Miss it and you get the answer, the reason, and the source; it comes back a few cards later and has to be answered right twice in a row to retire. Miss it twice and it shows up as a study card first. Work the count at the top down to zero, then take exam sims.

The exam. 150 questions, 3.5 hours, proctored at a Kryterion testing center or a NADCA event, not online. You need an active ASCS to sit it. To register, get a voucher from NADCA HQ (1-855-GO-NADCA, membership@nadca.com), then book the seat. NADCA does not publish the cut score; 72% is the working line here. Renewal is every 3 years by June 30.

How CVI differs from ASCS. ASCS asks how you clean it. CVI asks what you look at, what you measure, what you write down, what you recommend, and what you are liable for. Read every question from the inspector's seat.

Suggested order

1. Inspection to zero. It is 40% of the exam.
2. HVAC Systems to zero. This is the HVAC 101 that blindsided the ASCS sit: VAV and fan-powered boxes, multizone, dual duct, economizers, heat pumps, drain traps.
3. Everything else to zero, weakest domain first.
4. 30-question sims until you get two in a row above 85%.
5. One full 150 before you sit, for pacing.

Traps that cost points

10% is an inspection figure. Verification covers every specified component, and an S520 remediation job inspects all of them.
Recommended is not required. Hazmat consultation, ASCS oversight, routine inspection schedules, and the open air plenum section are recommendations or permissions. The written assessment, the work plan, and Level 1 containment are shall.
The Standard never defers. "At the client's option", "may be waived", "the IEP decides" are almost always wrong.
Method 3 is non-porous only. On liner and duct board, Method 2 is the end of the road.
Never promise. No health outcome and no energy savings figure goes in an inspection report.

Numbers that get tested

Inspect at least 10% of components (100% under S520). AHUs yearly for every building type; ducts every 2 years residential and marine, yearly for commercial, industrial and healthcare. Level 2: two-layer 6-mil floor 6 in. up the wall, 4 ACH scrubbing, manometer. Method 2: 4 brush passes, 80 in. static lift, 2.5 in. brush on a 1.5 in. hose. Method 3: 15 L/min pump set to 17 ±2, two passes per slot, pass at 0.75 mg/100 cm² or less. Fire dampers tested 1 year after install then every 4 years, hospitals every 6. Fall protection at 4 ft in general industry, 6 ft in construction. Ladders 3 ft past the landing at 4:1.

Where the questions come from

NADCA builds the CVI from its HVAC Inspection Manual and CVI course handouts, both member-only, plus ACR. This bank is built from ACR 2025 (checked line by line against the PDF), the primary texts of ASHRAE 62.1, NFPA 80, 90A, 96 and 105, SMACNA, UL, the IMC and IRC, OSHA and EPA, and the after-action from a passing ASCS sit. Every question shows its source after you answer. Every key and wrong answer was re-checked by a reviewer that did not write it, and the answer sets were audited so the right one cannot be picked from its length or wording. Progress saves in this browser only, so use the same phone or laptop each time.
