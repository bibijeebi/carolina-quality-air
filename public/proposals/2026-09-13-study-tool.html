Proposal № 012
The Study Tool

Sunday, September 13, 2026 · The ASCS study tool Ben passed with, and what it could become · Decision needed: who owns it and how far it goes
Bottom line

Ben passed the ASCS on September 2 off a study tool he built over the summer. It is live at carolinaqualityair.xyz/learning/ascs, it holds 925 questions across the six domains on the 2026 exam blueprint, and Thomas is studying off it now for his own sit. NADCA gives a candidate a standard, a $325 manual and a training course that NADCA itself says does not teach to the exam. Nothing in the industry works the way this does, and every one of NADCA's thousand-plus member companies has to keep at least one certified tech on staff. The ask is not money. It is a decision on who owns the tool and whether it stays a CQA training aid or gets finished into something other companies pay for. Cash needed from CQA right now: $0.

1 · What exists today

Facts about the tool as it sits, so the decision is about something real.

ItemWhere it stands

4 · Who else needs it

NADCA reported more than 1,000 member companies with over 1,700 certified ASCS holders and roughly 5,000 technicians across those companies. Membership requires keeping a certified tech on staff, so every one of those companies is a repeat customer for the exam whenever a cert holder leaves, and a lot of them would rather certify the whole crew than hang the company on one guy. Certification-exam prep is a normal business in every other trade; ham radio has HamStudy, the electricians and HVAC installers have a dozen apps each. Duct cleaning has nothing. The bar is on the floor.

What we copy from HamStudy

A study mode that tracks what you have seen and where you are weak and pushes you there, an explanation on every question, progress that follows you from phone to laptop, and one thing they built for ham clubs that is our whole commercial angle: sharing your progress with an instructor. For us that is the owner seeing where each tech stands.

What we don't copy

Their price. HamStudy is free with a $4 app because an antenna company pays for it and the question pool is public. Ours cost real hours to write and audit, and it gets re-audited every time the standard revs. It is sold per credential and per seat, with a free taste up front.

5 · What it takes to hand it to someone outside CQA

Right now progress lives in one phone's browser and nobody but the tech can see it. To be something another company would pay for, or even something Jeff can check on Thomas with, it needs the following, in this order. All of it is Ben's time; none of it is cash.

  1. Accounts. A login, so progress syncs across devices and survives a phone swap. Thomas's progress moves over on his first login.
  2. A placement test first. Thirty questions before any drilling, then the tool works the weakest domain first. A tech who already knows containment should not spend an hour on it.
  3. Real spaced review. Replace "twice in a row" with the scheduling method flashcard apps use, so the questions come back on the day they are about to be forgotten.
  4. Lessons in front of the drill, mostly on how HVAC systems are built, with our own diagrams. The from-nothing candidate needs this; Ben didn't, a new hire will.
  5. The owner's view. One line per tech: readiness, last active, practice exams taken. This is the page Jeff opens.
  6. A readiness light. Ben's rule of thumb is one data point. Every practice score logged against a real pass or fail turns it into a calibrated number. Ben is data point one, Thomas is two.
  7. Installs to the phone and works offline, without an app store. Then the other banks, one at a time.

Any of the four works. They differ in who owns it, who pays for the hours, and who gets the money if outside companies ever buy seats.

Ben's recommendation: B, today

It costs CQA nothing, it changes nothing about how the tool has been built so far, and it needs no decision beyond "keep going." CQA gets every tech certified off it for free, forever, and gets to say so. If outside companies ever actually pay for seats, C or D can be revisited then, with real numbers instead of guesses. A is the wrong fit only because the company would be paying tech-labor hours for software work, which is a poor trade for a duct cleaning company on a busy fall.

8 · How it goes

  1. Jeff picks A, B, C or D. "Keep going" means B. Nothing on this page needs a check written.
  2. Thomas is the pilot. He studies off the current tool, his practice scores get logged, he sits at the Cary center on his voucher. His pass is the second data point and the first case study.
  3. Ben finishes section 5 on his own time, in order: accounts, placement, review scheduling, lessons, the owner's view, the readiness light. Jeff gets the owner's view the day it exists.
  4. Two or three friendly NC member companies get it free for their next candidates in exchange for their scores and a quote. The 2027 Fall Technical Conference is in Concord, North Carolina, September 17 to 19, which puts the whole state's member companies in one room an hour and a half from Cary.
  5. Price it and take it to the room. NADCA's 38th Annual Meeting is April 4 to 7, 2027, at the Renaissance Orlando at SeaWorld. A laptop and a pass rate is the whole pitch.

9 · Reality check

NADCA will not buy this and should not be asked to. An association that certifies cannot also own the thing that teaches to its exam without wrecking its own credential; best case from them is a listed resource or an exhibitor table. The customers are their members, and that is a small market with zero competition, which is a strange but real place to be. The copyright posture is the other thing to get right the first time: the questions are ours, the standard is theirs, the tool assumes you own the ACR and sends you to NADCA's store for it. Best case at ninety percent: it never makes real money, but CQA becomes the company where every tech is certified, has a case study with numbers for the next Fall Tech, and never wastes another exam seat. That is worth Ben's evenings on its own.
