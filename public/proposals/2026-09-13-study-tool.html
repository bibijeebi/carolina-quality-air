Proposal № 012
The Study Tool

Sunday, September 13, 2026 · The HamStudy model, built for the ASCS and proven on Ben; what NADCA charges instead; and the four ways it could exist · Decision needed: who owns it and how far it goes
Bottom line

Every trade with a certification exam has a study app, and the one that proves the model is HamStudy: a question pool, a study mode that tracks what you have seen and where you are weak, an explanation on every miss, unlimited practice exams, progress an instructor can see, free to the candidate, and paid for by a manufacturer that wants its name in front of every new licensee. Ben passed two amateur radio license exams back to back after one afternoon on it. NADCA has nothing like it. A member company pays $685 to $1,210 in NADCA fees per ASCS candidate for a manual, a course NADCA's own registration page says does not teach to the exam, and a five-question sample sheet from 2016, then sends the tech in blind. So Ben built the HamStudy model for the ASCS on his own time, studied off it for about three days of real effort, and passed on September 2. It is live at carolinaqualityair.xyz/learning/ascs with 925 questions, and Thomas is on it now. The ask is not money. It is a decision on who owns the tool and whether it stays a CQA training aid or gets finished into the thing the other thousand member companies use. Cash needed from CQA right now: $0.

1 · How exam prep works when it works

HamStudy is the reference. It has been the default way to pass an amateur radio license exam for over a decade, it has a 4.9-star app with thousands of ratings, and it is sponsored by Icom, a radio manufacturer, because it works. The loop is simple and every piece of it matters.

What HamStudy is, in facts

Free on the website, $3.99 for the offline app, sponsored by Icom. Study Mode tracks progress through the questions, what you have seen, what you have not, and where you are struggling, and focuses your study on the questions you need to see most. Vetted explanations on every question. Infinite practice exams. English and Spanish pools. Multiple users on one device, progress synced to a free account, and progress you can share with friends, instructors or other users. It does not contain the instructional material from the license manuals; it works with the book on the table next to you.

Ben's experience with it

Two amateur radio license exams, taken back to back at one session, after a single afternoon in HamStudy. Each exam draws from a pool of several hundred questions. Ben did not read the manual first. The study mode found what he did not know, the explanations taught it, and the practice exams said when he was ready. That is the whole method, and it is the method this proposal copies.

NATE is the proof that HVAC techs pay for this

NATE, the big HVAC technician certification, charges $136 for its Core exam and $146 per Specialty, and NATE itself tells candidates that its topic outlines "don't teach the concepts," to cross-reference them against any reference book, and to find a training provider. So a whole market grew up around NATE: study guides, practice-test apps and courses selling for anywhere from $35 to over $1,000, plus NATE's own paid training academy run by an outside company. Same trade, same kind of tech, same "here's the outline, good luck" from the certifying body, and people pay to fill the gap. Around NADCA's exam, which costs nearly three times as much before the course and travel, nothing has been built beyond the dump sites.

4 · The exams it could cover

The engine does not care what the questions are about; each exam is a separate bank. Ben's plan is to build all four before anything is sold, for two reasons: nobody would use HamStudy if it only had the Technician pool, and writing the bank is how you learn an exam cold. In order:

Fits the dryer-vent line the door hangers were drafted around, and the same bank sells to chimney sweeps, a bigger pool than duct cleaners.
CVI, inspectionNADCA150 Q, 3.5 hr, five domains, inspection is 40%Requires an active ASCS first, renews every three years. Nobody at CQA holds one today. Smallest audience of the four; last unless inspection work comes back.

Not affiliated with or endorsed by NADCA, CSIA or HamStudy. ASCS, CVI and ACR are NADCA's marks; C-DET is CSIA's; HamStudy is Signal Stuff's. Anything we publish is prep for their exams, written in our own words, and points people to the issuing body's store for the standards themselves.

5 · Who else needs it

NADCA has reported more than 1,000 member companies with over 1,700 certified ASCS holders and roughly 5,000 technicians across them. Membership requires keeping a certified tech on staff, so every one of those companies buys the exam again whenever a cert holder leaves, and plenty would rather certify the crew than hang the company on one guy. HamStudy exists because that same situation in ham radio, a pool of questions and a body that only publishes the pool, was worth an antenna company sponsoring a study app for a decade. Duct cleaning has the pool, the fee, the turnover, and nothing else.

What we copy: the product

The study mode that tracks what you have seen and where you are weak and pushes you there, an explanation on every question, progress that follows you from phone to laptop, multiple users on one device, and the feature they built for ham clubs that is our whole angle: sharing your progress with an instructor. For us the instructor is the owner.

What we copy: the business

HamStudy is free on the web and $3.99 for the offline app. The instructor features are free. Icom pays to have its name on the thing every new ham stares at for a week, and that is the revenue. Ours works the same way: free to every tech in the country, a few dollars for the app, the owner's view free, and a NADCA associate member, the vacuum, whip and chemical vendors who buy a booth at every Fall Tech, paying to be the name on it. Every new ASCS candidate in the industry, about to start buying equipment for a career, staring at one sponsor's logo for three days. That is the same trade Icom made, and it is worth more than seat sales ever will be in a market this size.

The one place the copy breaks: HamStudy's question pool is public, so their content costs nothing to write and revs itself every four years. Ours is written and audited by hand and revs with the ACR. That is what the sponsor money funds, and it is the line in the sponsor pitch: you are paying to keep the bank current, and your name is on it.

6 · What it takes to hand it to someone outside CQA

Right now progress lives in one phone's browser and nobody but the tech can see it. To be something another company would pay for, or even something Jeff can check on Thomas with, it needs the following, in this order. All of it is Ben's time; none of it is cash.

  1. Accounts. A login, so progress syncs across devices and survives a phone swap. Thomas's progress moves over on his first login.
  2. A placement test first. Thirty questions before any drilling, then the tool works the weakest domain first.
  3. Real spaced review. Replace "twice in a row" with the scheduling that flashcard apps use, so questions come back the day they are about to be forgotten.
  4. Lessons in front of the drill, mostly on how HVAC systems are built, with our own diagrams. Ben didn't need them; a new hire will.
  5. The owner's view. One line per tech: readiness, last active, practice exams taken. This is the page Jeff opens.
  6. A readiness light. Ben's rule of thumb is one data point. Every practice score logged against a real pass or fail turns it into a number. Ben is data point one, Thomas is two.
  7. Installs to the phone and works offline, without an app store. Then the other banks, one at a time.

9 · How it goes

  1. Jeff picks A, B, C or D. "Keep going" means B. Nothing on this page needs a check written.
  2. Thomas is the pilot. He studies off the current tool, his practice scores get logged, he sits at the Cary center on his voucher. His pass is the second data point and the first case study.
  3. Ben finishes section 6 on his own time, in order, and writes the CVI, dryer and Spanish banks alongside. Jeff gets the owner's view the day it exists.
  4. Two or three friendly NC member companies get it free for their next candidates in exchange for their scores and a quote. The 2027 Fall Technical Conference is in Concord, North Carolina, September 17 to 19, which puts the state's member companies in one room an hour and a half from Cary.
  5. Take it to the room. NADCA's 38th Annual Meeting is April 4 to 7, 2027, at the Renaissance Orlando at SeaWorld, where every associate member has a booth. A laptop, a pass rate and Thomas's numbers is the whole sponsor pitch. Per-seat pricing only comes out if nobody bites.

10 · Reality check

NADCA will not buy this and should not be asked to. A body that certifies cannot also own the thing that teaches to its exam without wrecking its own credential; best case from them is a listed resource or an exhibitor table, which is also exactly where HamStudy sits with the ham radio bodies. The users are their members and the customer is a vendor: a small market with no real competition, which is a strange but real place to be, and exactly the place HamStudy has occupied for a decade. The copyright posture is the other thing to get right the first time, and HamStudy's own disclaimer is the model: the questions are ours, the standard is theirs, the tool assumes you own the ACR and sends you to NADCA's store for it. Best case at ninety percent: it never makes real money, but CQA becomes the company where every tech is certified, has a case study with numbers for the next Fall Tech, and never wastes another exam seat. That is worth Ben's evenings on its own.
