Proposal № 013
The Van Wallet

Thursday, September 17, 2026 · One pouch per van: the key on a tracker, a fuel-only card, the wash card, and a note that says what each one is for · About $150 in hardware, fuel cards from $0 a month
Bottom line

Right now the van keys, the wash cards, and the company card float between people and trucks. Nobody is sure which card is allowed to buy what, so a driver who is low on fuel on the way to Greenville either calls Jeff or drives it down to the last 30 miles of range. The fix is the same one every fleet uses: each van gets its own pouch that never leaves the van, with the key on an AirTag ring, a fuel card that can only buy fuel, the wash card, and a laminated line that says exactly what each card is for. The fuel card gives Jeff more control than he has today, not less: every gallon is tied to a van and a driver PIN, limits are set per card, and the whole thing shows up on one statement. Hardware is about $150 for the fleet. The fuel card program is $4 a month per active driver, or free on the Shell program if Jeff would rather trade a little acceptance for zero fees. Nothing here requires a new line of credit beyond the fuel card itself.

2 · The pouch

One bright zip pouch per van, labeled with the van number, kept in the same spot in every van (the driver's door pocket is the obvious one). It holds: the van key on a ring with an AirTag in a locking holder; the fleet fuel card for that van; the wash card and any gate or site badges; a copy of the registration and insurance card; and a laminated card that says in plain words what the fuel card can and cannot buy and who to call if the pump declines it. The pouch belongs to the van, not the driver. When a driver swaps vans, he swaps pouches. Nothing gets handed person to person, which is the actual cause of the floating.

The AirTag does two jobs. It finds the key when it walks off, and it finds the van in a hotel lot or a big site. Any iPhone on the crew can see it in Find My once the tags are shared to a company Apple ID or to Jeff's. One honest limit: an AirTag only reports when an iPhone passes near it, so a key left in a rural customer's driveway can go quiet for a day. That is still a lot better than no signal.

3 · The fuel card

A fleet fuel card is a different product from a company credit card. It is built for the control side: fuel-only by default, a driver PIN at the pump, per-card limits on gallons and dollars, hours of operation, an odometer prompt so mileage tracking comes free, and real-time text alerts on anything unusual. For CQA's territory, Raleigh to Greenville to whatever base or courthouse is next, universal acceptance matters more than the extra pennies. Coast and WEX both work at essentially every pump; Fuelman's network is where the rural gaps show up. Between Coast and WEX, Coast is simpler to run from a phone and publishes exactly which fees it does not charge. At four vans, Coast costs about $16 a month and the 3–9¢ rebate on a normal month's fuel comes close to covering it. The Shell program is the fallback if the answer to any monthly fee is no.

Fleet card or rewards card? Fleet cards pay in cents per gallon, which works out to about 2% back, and none of them have a points program. Ordinary business credit cards pay more on fuel: Costco Anywhere Visa Business 4% on gas (needs the membership), U.S. Bank Triple Cash 3% with no annual fee, Ink Business Cash 2% on gas and 5% on office and phone, and the hotel cards (IHG, Hilton, Marriott business) 4–5x points at gas stations, which turns fuel into free rooms on overnight jobs. What those cards cannot do is lock a card to fuel only or ask for a PIN and odometer at the pump; they give per-employee limits and alerts instead. On roughly $2,400 a month of fleet fuel the gap between 2% and 4% is about $50 a month. The recommendation on this page is both: the fleet card in every van pouch for control, and one rewards business card as the company card for parts and supplies, which is the card that replaces the one under Nick's name in section 5.

Two things to ask before signing any of them. First, who signs the personal guarantee: fleet cards for a company this size usually want an owner's guarantee, and that should be Jeff or Perry, never an employee. Second, whether CQA's existing bank has a fleet card of its own; sometimes the answer is already sitting in the business checking account.

The company-card line is the only real judgment call on this page. The case for it is simple: every time a tech can't spend $80 in the company's interest without a phone call, that is Jeff's time and a job that runs slower. Employee cards with per-card limits are the normal answer and they show up on one statement with the receipts attached. If Jeff would rather keep that to one or two people, the fuel card alone still removes the most common call.

5 · The company card cleanup

The card in circulation is still under Nick's personal Social Security number. That means a company account is reporting on his personal credit, and if anything ever went wrong with it, he is the one on the hook. The clean fix is to open the replacement card under CQA's EIN with Jeff or Perry as the guarantor, move the recurring charges over, and close the old account so Nick's name comes off it. This is the same item flagged in № 008 under employee cards; it belongs here because the new fuel cards should be set up under the company from day one rather than added to the old account.

7 · Steps

  1. Jeff picks the fuel program (Coast, WEX, or Shell) and signs as guarantor. Ben fills out the application and sets the per-card limits Jeff wants. Cards arrive in about a week.
  2. Ben buys the hardware, pairs the four AirTags to Jeff's Apple ID (or a CQA Apple ID), names them Van 1 through Van 4, and shares them to the crew's phones.
  3. Build the pouches on the next Sunday reset: key on the AirTag ring, fuel card, wash card, registration copy, rules card. Label the pouch and its home in each van.
  4. Ten-minute crew brief: the pouch stays with the van, PIN at the pump, what the fuel card buys, who to call if it declines. Same words as the laminated card.
  5. Reissue the company card under the EIN, move the recurring charges, close the account tied to Nick, and decide on employee cards for Ben and Thomas.
