4 · How the switch works

Plain English, and the key fact up front: the domain already belongs to CQA. It was registered in 2003, long before the current agency existed. Agencies manage sites; the name is ours.

  1. Jeff asks Red Shark the question. “Whose account is the carolinaqualityair.com domain registered under — ours or yours? And what’s our monthly total with you?” One email or one phone call.
  2. If it’s CQA’s account: we get the login (or recover it through the email on file) and Red Shark never needs to be involved again.
  3. If it’s theirs: Jeff sends one written line: “Please move carolinaqualityair.com to our account.” This is routine agency offboarding — registrars have a standard free process for it, agencies handle these requests every month, and the client is considered the domain’s owner regardless of whose account holds it. No drama expected from a legitimate shop.
  4. Benny points the domain at the new site. An hour or two of technical work on his end. The old site keeps serving right up until the moment the new one takes over — zero downtime, nothing for customers to notice except a better site.
  5. Same day: the new site is live on carolinaqualityair.com, quote-form leads start landing in the office inbox, and Google is told the site exists.
  6. Cleanup: cancel the agency arrangement and the old platform’s hosting fee. That’s money back against the retainer immediately.

5 · What “everything included” means after the switch

Any change — a new photo, a price note, a new service page, a seasonal banner — is a text message, handled same day, no ticket queue, no hourly invoice. Every quote-form lead is emailed instantly and kept on file, so nothing lives only in a voicemail. And the domain becomes the home for everything already being built on it: the lead reports, the repair write-ups, and this proposals board all move with it. One flat number covers the whole web presence.

6 · The honest risks

Two, and both are small. First, the switch depends on Red Shark answering a routine offboarding request — if they drag their feet, the old site simply keeps running while it gets sorted; nothing breaks in the meantime. Second, this puts the web presence on one person. The mitigation is that everything lives in CQA’s name — the domain in CQA’s account, the leads filed where the office can see them — so the arrangement is reversible by design. The old site isn’t deleted on day one, and pointing the domain back at it takes an hour. That’s a smaller risk than the status quo, where the presence sits with an agency that’s left filler text on the homepage for years.
