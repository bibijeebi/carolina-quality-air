Proposal № 011
The Training House

Saturday, September 12, 2026 · Ben's house as the job site: training, a check ride, and content, with no customer and no clock · Decision needed: which of the three, and when
Bottom line

Every job we run has a customer in the kitchen and a clock on the wall, so nobody gets to slow down, do a step twice, or film it. Ben's house is a real dirty residential job, the exact house the mailer just went to 2,171 of, and it is ours for a day. The idea is to use it three ways, together or separately: a slow training day where every man does every station; a check ride where Ben runs the whole residential job start to finish on his own so Jeff and Perry can see he can be sent to a house alone; and a content day where Bekah documents all of it for the website and the written procedure. Nothing gets invoiced, the crew is on their normal terms, and Ben's own hours are free because a cleaned house is his end of it. Money is the easy part and gets settled after the format is picked. The idea is the ask.

1 · The idea

Three reasons this house and not a customer's. It is the job we sell most and just mailed for: a 2000s one-story on a crawl with flex duct and a furnace under the floor, so most of the calls that come off the September mailer will be this house. Nobody is paying, so nobody is watching the clock: a new guy can cut his first return cleanout twice, a patch can be done wrong and then right, the camera can be reset between every register. And it is Ben's, so the photos, the crawl footage and the mistakes all belong to us and can go straight into the website and the procedures without asking anybody's permission.

Run the check ride first, on its own morning, filmed. If Ben passes it, the crew day is taught by someone who has just proven the whole job alone, and the video from the check ride is already most of C. If he doesn't, the list of what he got wrong is exactly what the crew day should drill. Either way A happens second and Bekah shoots both.

3 · The house

What's there

Willow Spring, south Wake County. Built 2006, one story, 1,814 sq ft, 10 ft ceilings, two-car garage. One split system: propane furnace horizontal in a vented crawl space with the coil on it, flex duct, eleven 4x12 floor registers, two wall returns with 20x20 filters. Electric dryer in a laundry room off the garage, four-inch collar low on the wall. Half the crawl is a belly crawl, half is a crouch, torn plastic on the dirt. 200-amp panel outside. The driveway held all three vans on the August 30 gear day.

Its condition

Never professionally cleaned since it was built. When Ben shop-vacced the registers in 2025 several runs were heavily loaded, one looked pulled partway off its collar, and the closet register had what looked like mold underneath. Large dog in the house. It is a real dirty job, not a staged one. The 2007 R-22 condenser is being replaced next year under a state program and stays out of scope, as does mastic-sealing the crawl runs, which that program may cover. No fiberglass-lined duct here, so encapsulation stays a commercial-job lesson.

4 · The job, in order

This is the residential job as we run it, and it is the checklist for all three formats: what the crew learns, what Ben is graded on, and what Bekah shoots a before, during and after of.

  1. Walk and count. Furnace and crawl hatch found, eleven supplies and two returns counted, runs traced to rooms, every register face photographed before it's touched.
  2. Containment and setup. Plastic under registers and paths, corner guards, dog out. Neg-air machine outside the hatch exhausting away from the house, collector hose through the hatch to the return trunk.
  3. Return side first. Grilles and filters out, cleanout cut on the return trunk, machine running, returns whipped back to the trunk.
  4. Supply side from the boots. Eleven registers, the standard whip pass from the boot and the reverse pass from the trunk. Soft rod in flex and around 90s, hard rod on straights only.
  5. Cut, plug and patch in the crawl. Access cut where the whip can't reach, closed right: pop plugs, sheet-metal patch, mastic, insulation and FSK back. The pulled run re-hung and sealed at the collar.
  6. Coil last. Horizontal furnace coil in a crawl, the awkward one. Cleaner, degreaser, washer, pan and drain checked.
  7. Grilles at the pressure washer, laid out to dry in the order they came off.
  8. Dryer vent. Run measured and elbows counted against the code allowance, cleaned from the hood end, damper checked, joints back with the airflow, dryer run and flow felt at the hood. This becomes the dryer-vent card the door hangers promise.
  9. Fog.
  10. Closeout and the customer walk. New filters, registers on straight, plastic up, floors swept, after photos beside the befores. Then the Perry walkthrough with Bekah as the homeowner, on camera.
  11. If the garage mini split is hung by then, a head cleaning is one more station.

5 · What it costs, in one paragraph

No revenue on the day and nothing invoiced. Ben's own hours are free; the cleaned house is his pay. The crew is at their rate, or the Sunday time-and-a-half terms Jeff already set for the gear days. Bekah is on the same van-day terms. Consumables are about $60 for a crew day and about $40 for a Ben-only day. One van. That is the whole cost, and the exact numbers get written up once Jeff picks the format and the crew.

6 · How it goes

  1. Jeff picks the format and the day. A, B, C or any combination, and which guys. Sundays September 20, September 27 and October 4 all work on Ben's end.
  2. Ben stages the house: dog out, cars out, hatch cleared, plastic pulled back on the hose path, filters bought, camera batteries charged.
  3. The van comes down loaded per the standard load.
  4. Run the job in order. Slow for A, real pace for B. Bekah shoots. Ben keeps the log of what went right, wrong and missing from the van, the same as the gear days.
  5. Within the week: the written procedure and van card go out, the photos get paired, the check-ride verdict is written down, and the first cut of content goes to Jeff before anything is posted.

7 · Reality check

The crawl is the crawl: half belly crawl, slow, muddy, unpopular, which is exactly why it's worth training in and worth proving in. The day will run long the first time and nothing is riding on it. The only way this goes wrong is if it gets treated like a job with a customer in the house and everyone rushes. The check ride is the one piece with a real downside, a no, and that is the point of doing it here: a no at Ben's house costs a Sunday; a no at a customer's costs the customer.
