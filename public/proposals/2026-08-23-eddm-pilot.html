Proposal № 001
Direct Mail Pilot

Sunday, August 23, 2026 · SE Raleigh duct-age neighborhoods · Decision needed: yes/no on ~$650
Bottom line For about $650, the mailer you’ve seen goes to roughly 1,500 homes in the single best cluster of 15–20-year-old houses in SE Raleigh — the neighborhoods from the rundown you asked for, right around Marcus and Devin. USPS delivers it to every door on the selected postal routes; no mailing list, no contractor, no ongoing commitment. One booked whole-home cleaning roughly pays for the entire run. If it produces jobs, the same play scales to the full zone (~3,500 homes, ~$1,350) and repeats seasonally.

1 · Where this came from

On August 20 you asked for a rundown of neighborhoods around Benny’s, Marcus’s, and Devin’s addresses where most homes are 15–20 years old. We built it from Wake County assessor parcel data (not listing-site guesses): 52 ranked subdivisions, full report here.

Why this age band buys duct cleaning: homes built 2006–2011 are on original builder-grade flex duct and panned returns that have usually never been cleaned; original HVAC equipment is at or past end of life, so coils and protecting a new system are live topics; many are on their second or third owner; and mid-2000s construction usually means construction debris has been sealed in the runs since day one.

3 · The piece

This is the design from the August 23 text thread — the one that got the “those look great.” That was a read on the look, not a spend decision; the money question is this page, kept separate on purpose.

6.5×9 postcard, 14pt gloss cover, printed both sides. The Wake County version carries the 919-907-9742 Cary number so it reads local, and the postage block is already formatted to USPS Every Door Direct Mail rules, so the post office accepts it as-is. QR goes to carolinaqualityair.com. The offer — free dryer vent inspection with any duct cleaning — needs whoever answers the 919 line to recognize it, or the money burns twice.

5 · What happens on “go”

  1. Lock the routes (10 minutes, same day). On the USPS EDDM map, select the carrier routes containing Kernstown Dr, Burtons Barn St, Chasteal Trl, and Heather Ridge Ln. The tool shows the exact piece count and exact postage to the penny. That number replaces the estimates above before anything is spent.
  2. Order the print (same day). Upload the finished Wake County file (919 number) to the print shop: 6.5×9, 14pt gloss cover, quantity per step 1. Ships in about 4–6 business days.
  3. Prep the drop (1 evening). Bundle in stacks of 100, all facing the same way, rubber-banded, one facing slip per bundle (the USPS tool prints them), plus the one-page USPS form the tool generates.
  4. Drop and pay. Take the boxes to the post office that serves those routes, pay postage at the counter. USPS delivers to every door on the routes within about 7–10 business days.
  5. Brief the phone. Whoever answers 919-907-9742 knows the free dryer vent inspection offer exists and honors it. One sentence to the office covers it.

6 · How we know if it worked

Three signals, all free: calls to the 919 number that mention the card or the dryer vent offer; QR scans hitting the website; and a “how’d you hear about us” on every booking during the delivery window. Honest expectations: cold saturation mail usually returns a fraction of a percent, so 1,500 pieces means a handful of calls, not a flood. The math works anyway — one booked whole-home cleaning roughly covers the pilot, and every card after that is profit plus a house that now knows our name sits in their neighborhood.

7 · The trigger to scale

If the pilot books 2 or more jobs, run the full-zone sweep (~$1,350, ~3,500 homes) and put the drop on a seasonal clock: fall furnace startup and spring allergy season are the two windows when this offer reads strongest. If it books zero, we’re out one pilot and we know, and the door-hanger lane keeps running at walking-around money either way.
