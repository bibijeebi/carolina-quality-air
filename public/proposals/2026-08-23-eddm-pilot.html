Proposal № 001
Direct Mail Pilot

Proposed August 23, 2026 · Ordered August 29, 2026 · SE Raleigh duct-age neighborhoods · 2,171 homes · $887.74 all in
Bottom line The mailer you approved goes to every mailbox on three postal routes in SE Raleigh: 2,171 homes, about 830 of them built 2006–2011. Total cost is $887.74, print plus postage, no middleman. The print shop’s quote was $800–900 for one neighborhood; this covers three. One booked whole-home cleaning pays for the entire run. Print is ordered; cards land Sep 3–10 and go in the mail the same week. One-page PDF of this plan.

1 · The card, as ordered

Same design from the August 23 thread, one change: the tagline now reads “Cleaning Up Your Indoor Air Pollution.” The logo file on carolinaqualityair.com spells it with one L, and that’s probably what’s on the trucks. Fixing the source logo is a separate decision; the card just shouldn’t carry a typo into 2,171 mailboxes.

2 · Where it goes and why

Routes come from the neighborhood rundown you asked for on Aug 20 (Wake County parcel data, 52 ranked subdivisions): homes built 2006–2011 sit on original builder flex duct that has usually never been cleaned, with original equipment at end of life. USPS sells mail by carrier route, not by subdivision, so every 2006–2011 home in 27610 was matched to its carrier route and the routes were ranked by target density and household income.

Mailing the whole ZIP blind would land on a 2006–2011 home about 10% of the time; these three routes hit 38%. They are also the three highest-income routes in 27610, the households that book a $1,500–1,800 job off a postcard. The other 62% of the mailboxes are the same streets and the same income, mostly 1995–2005 and 2012–2018 homes whose ducts nobody has cleaned either.

Checked against the rest of the recon area: the best route in 27592 (Willow Spring) is 15% target homes and the best in 27604 is 16%, which is why those zones get door hangers, not mail. A third route (C039, Chasteal Trails, 25%) was left off because it would push the run past the cards on hand.

2,500 is the printer’s smallest tier above 1,000 at this size and two 1,000 orders cost more. The 329 spare cards go to walk-ins and drops around the first jobs this books. No mailing list, no mail house, no design fee, no permit. Print is on the company card as of Aug 29; postage is paid online the day the cards are dropped.

4 · What happens next

  1. Cards arrive at Ben’s house, Sep 3–10. Printer sends a PDF proof first; Ben approves it same day.
  2. Ben bundles them to USPS spec. 100 per bundle, facing slips, PS Form 3587, all generated from the USPS order already saved (order “pilot,” 2,171 pieces).
  3. Postage paid online, boxes dropped at the Sunnybrook post office. One trip; all three routes deliver out of that station. Carriers deliver within the week.
  4. Brief the phone. Whoever answers 919-907-9742 knows the free dryer vent inspection with any duct cleaning exists and honors it. Ask every caller how they heard about us.

5 · How we know if it worked, and the trigger to scale

Three free signals during the delivery window: calls to the 919 number that mention the card or the dryer vent offer, QR scans on the website, and the “how’d you hear about us” answer on every booking. Honest expectations: cold saturation mail returns a fraction of a percent, so 2,171 pieces means a handful of calls, not a flood. One booked whole-home cleaning covers the run. If it books 2 or more jobs, the same play goes to the rest of the zone, about 3,500 more homes for roughly $1,300, on a seasonal clock (fall furnace startup, spring allergies). If it books zero, we are out one pilot and we know.
