Proposal № 015
The Ground Game

Friday, September 25, 2026 · Remodel permits, door hangers, lawn signs and the call-source question, run as one local program · about $120–215 in cash
Bottom line

Every remodel in the Triangle needs a building permit, and the permits are public. Five county and city data feeds list 1,627 residential remodel, addition and repair permits issued in our target ZIPs in the last 90 days, 155 of them in the last week alone. Every one of those houses has drywall dust and construction debris in the ductwork, and the owner is about to move back in. Ben pulls that list automatically every Monday morning and works it with door hangers on the remodel house and both neighbors.

Jeff's lawn signs go where the law lets them stay: at our own job sites, and in Fuquay-Varina yards with the owner's OK. Roadside corners in Raleigh are the best spots and they're banned outright, so that part is Jeff's call. Every sign carries CQA's number. The cash side is door hangers, stakes and a $10 town permit. The rest is Ben's time and the van. The last piece costs nothing: Vicki asks every caller how they heard about us, so a month from now we know which of these rang the phone.

1 · Already in motion

The mailer is in homes. The № 001 postcard went to 2,171 homes on three SE Raleigh postal routes (27610, routes C001, C004 and C032) on September 5, through the Sunnybrook post office. Those routes cover Battle Ridge North, Griffis Glen and Tryon Place, the best cluster from the August neighborhood study. 338 spare cards are on hand.

The neighborhood study. In August Ben ranked 52 subdivisions near the crew's homes where most houses were built 2006–2011, from Wake County's parcel records. Those houses have original builder-grade duct and HVAC equipment at the end of its life. 33 are in SE Raleigh and 19 are in the Willow Spring and Fuquay-Varina corridor.

The signs. Jeff is supplying lawn signs. Ben has 100 placements mapped and routed, so whatever count Jeff hands over goes out from the top of that list.

2 · Remodel permits: the new lead list

A remodel is the best moment to sell duct cleaning. Sanding drywall, cutting flooring and demolishing walls puts fine dust straight into the returns while the system runs. The homeowner is already spending money on the house and wants it clean when they move back in. The permit record tells us the address, the date, what's being done and often the contractor.

How Ben works it

Three hangers per remodel: the house and the neighbors on each side, who watched the dumpster for weeks too. The target is permits issued three to eight weeks back, when the dirty work is finishing and the owner is about to move back in. If the owner is home, Ben asks to stake a sign by the dumpster, which is the lowest-risk sign there is. On damage repairs he asks whether the insurance claim is still open.

The remodelers themselves

The same permits name the contractors. The ones pulling the most permits in our ZIPs right now include EZ Pro Construction Group and EZPro Kitchen & Bath, Frey's Building and Remodeling, The Bath Remodeling Center, Madison Renovations, Power Home Remodeling and Sigmon Construction. Restoration outfits like Carolina Restoration Services of NC show up on the damage jobs. The crawlspace companies (Groundworks North Carolina, Carolina Basement Systems) are in homes with ducts under the floor every day. A remodeler who puts "post-construction duct cleaning" on the punch list sends us every job they finish. Ben would start calling these the same way Perry works the facility managers.

Blind spot: Apex, Holly Springs, Fuquay-Varina, Morrisville, Garner, Wake Forest, Chapel Hill, Carrboro and Clayton issue their own permits through search websites with no data feed. Those towns only show up when a house is outside town limits and the county issued the permit.

3 · Lawn signs: where they can legally go

The bandit signs on corners by stop signs are illegal almost everywhere around here, and cities pull them. Raleigh bans any sign in the street right-of-way, and also bans off-premise ads in private yards (UDO §7.3.15 and §7.3.2). State law bars commercial signs on NCDOT right-of-way, and DOT crews remove them. Most of the suburbs ban off-premise ads the same way. Three towns allow a small sign in a private yard with the owner's permission and no permit:

TownSign in a customer's or friend's yard
Fuquay-VarinaYes Owner's permission, 12 sq ft max per lot, 42" tall, up 14 days then moved (LDO §9-1556)
MorrisvilleYes 1 per lot, 6 sq ft, 90 days a year (UDO §5.16)
ZebulonYes 1 per lot, 6 sq ft, 10 ft off every lot line, no time limit (UDO §5.11.9)

Garner, KnightdaleUnclear Each allows one 3 sq ft yard sign but also bans off-premise ads
Raleigh, Cary, Apex, Wake Forest, Rolesville, Clayton, unincorporated Wake and JohnstonNo Off-premise ads banned

A sign at our own job site is on the lot where we're doing the work, with the owner standing there. None of these codes carve out that case by name, but it's the lowest-risk sign we can put out: the customer's yard for the length of the job, next to the van, pulled when we leave. Add three hangers on the neighbors, a photo of the sign and the van for the Google profile, and every residential job markets the street it's on.

4 · Door hanger rules

Hangers are the legal version of stuffing mailboxes, with a few rules:

RuleSource
Never on or in a mailbox, its post or its flag. Federal fine per piece.18 U.S.C. § 1725
Skip any house posted "No Soliciting" or "No Trespassing." In NC a posted sign is legal notice, and walking up after it is second-degree trespass.G.S. 14-159.13
Stay out of gated communities and apartment complexes without management's written OK.Private property
Fuquay-Varina: door-to-door salesmen need a town permit from the Police Department. $10, good for a year, work between 9 AM and 8 PM, carry it. Leaving a hanger without knocking may not count as soliciting, but $10 settles the question.Town Code §6-1041 to 6-1042.1
Raleigh: no door-to-door permit since July 2015, when NC ended city business licenses. Raleigh PD says to carry ID. Soliciting on the streets and sidewalks is banned, so talk at the door, not the curb.RPD notice, Nov 2015; City Code §12-1026
Hang it on the knob, never the porch floor or the glass. A hanger that blows into the yard is litter, and litter is what people complain about.Practice

6 · The October hook

The first cool mornings of fall, everyone turns the heat on for the first time and smells burning dust. Hangers and signs this month say it in under seven words: "Burning smell when the heat kicked on? Your ducts." with the phone number big. A driver at a stop sign has about three seconds.

7 · The van

A lettered van is a moving billboard, and it's legal while it's working. Parking it overnight at a Walmart or Lowe's just to be seen is not the play. Raleigh's sign code bans a signed vehicle parked to advertise to passing traffic (UDO §7.3.15 item 14). Stores tow or ticket overnight vehicles, and a van full of vacuums and compressors in an empty lot is a break-in waiting to happen. At jobs, park it where the street can see it. When driving to jobs anyway, take the target neighborhoods instead of the highway where it's close.

8 · Knowing what worked

None of this is worth repeating unless we know which part rang the phone. The fix costs nothing. Vicki asks every new caller "How did you hear about us?" and writes down one word: mailer, hanger, sign, van, Google, referral, other. After a month that column tells us whether the mailer paid for itself, whether hangers beat signs, and where the next $500 goes.
