Proposal № 008
Money on the Table

Friday, September 4, 2026 · Programs CQA already qualifies for · Decision needed: a yes on each, and one afternoon of paperwork
Bottom line None of these cost money. They are programs a twelve-person North Carolina contractor qualifies for by existing, ranked by dollars over effort. The first one alone can reimburse most of what the certifications cost. The ask is a yes on each line and the paperwork, which Ben does.

1 · Training reimbursement: up to $15,000 a year

The NCWorks Incumbent Worker Training Grant reimburses employers for the cost of training and certification exams for existing employees, and each qualified employer can be awarded up to $15,000 per year. It is administered through the local workforce development board (Pitt County is in the Region Q board's area) and is designed for exactly this: an employee needs a credential to keep or grow their job, the company pays for the class or exam, the state pays the company back.

What it covers at CQA: the NADCA ASCS exams for Thomas and Will, the CVI for Ben, the IKECA exam if the hood lane goes, the aerial lift and forklift classes, and any NADCA conference registration where the training is documented. Grants are awarded twice a year; the application goes in before the training, not after, so the next NADCA push is the first one to file for.

2 · Veteran hiring credit: $2,400 to $9,600 per hire

The federal Work Opportunity Tax Credit pays a business a tax credit for hiring from certain groups, and veterans are the largest one. A veteran hired off unemployment is worth $2,400; a service-disabled veteran unemployed six months or more is worth up to $9,600. It comes straight off the company's federal tax bill.

The catch is timing: IRS Form 8850 must be filed with the state workforce agency within 28 days of the hire's start date, so it only works if the form is part of the hiring packet. Ben was hired in December and that window is closed. Every future hire gets the form on day one. One veteran hire a year pays for a NADCA conference.

3 · A retirement plan that is nearly free for three years

Federal law (SECURE 2.0) pays employers with 50 or fewer employees a credit equal to 100% of plan startup and administration costs, up to $5,000 a year for three years, plus a credit of up to $1,000 per employee for employer contributions in the early years. CQA has no retirement plan today. A SIMPLE IRA or a small-business 401(k) through the payroll provider costs a few hundred to a couple thousand a year to run, all of which the credit covers, and matching contributions are largely covered too.

Why this matters more than the credit: it is the cheapest retention tool a family company can offer a tech who is deciding between CQA and the next guy. Nobody in duct cleaning offers one. The credit makes it free to be the one who does.

4 · Employee cards, and a card that pays back

One company card, one person holding it, and it ends up in Jeff's pocket while a tech buys gas on his own money and chases $37. Every real business card issues additional employee cards at no cost, each with its own number and its own monthly limit. Alternatives if the current card cannot: a Ramp account (free corporate card platform, a card per person, limits and merchant rules per card, receipts by text, 1.5% back) or a WEX or Fuelman fleet fuel card with per-driver PINs for the gas problem alone.

Second half: if the card the company runs on earns nothing, switching to a 1.5 to 2% business rewards card on $50,000+ of annual fuel, hotel, parts, and print spend is $750 to $1,500 a year in cash back for changing which card is in the wallet.

5 · Two questions for the insurance agent

  1. Workers' comp class code. Duct cleaning gets thrown into the wrong classification often, and the premium difference between codes on a twelve-person payroll can be thousands a year. One call to confirm the code and whether the auditor has the payroll split right.
  2. Fleet policy extras. Whether the commercial auto policy has a full-glass endorsement (it decides whether the cracked windshield on the van costs $0 or the deductible; North Carolina does not waive glass deductibles by law) and whether the carrier offers a telematics or fleet safety discount.

6 · The small ones

  1. Off-highway fuel tax. Gas burned in the pressure washer, generators, and gas-powered equipment is not road fuel. The federal excise refund is about 18 cents a gallon (Form 4136 with the return) and North Carolina refunds state motor fuel tax on off-highway use too. A few hundred a year if someone logs the gallons; Ben will.
  2. NC Cash. The state unclaimed property database at nccash.com. Search the company name and both owners' names. Two minutes, sometimes real money from an old deposit or overpayment.
  3. Supplier programs. Lowe's Pro rewards, Harbor Freight Inside Track, Amazon Business pricing. Free, and the Texas Roadhouse gift cards proved this stuff adds up.

7 · One thing to ask the accountant

Not a windfall, the opposite. North Carolina charges sales tax on "repair, maintenance, and installation services," and whether duct cleaning falls under it is not obvious from the statute. If CQA should be collecting sales tax and is not, that is a liability that grows every quarter. Five minutes with whoever does the books settles it.

8 · What happens on "go"

  1. This week. Employee cards ordered or Ramp set up. NC Cash searched. Agent called with the two insurance questions.
  2. Before the next certification push. Incumbent Worker Training Grant application filed through the workforce board with the training plan attached (ASCS x2, CVI, IKECA).
  3. Hiring packet. Form 8850 added to it permanently.
  4. Q4. Retirement plan quote from the payroll provider, plan starts January so the full first-year credit lands on the 2027 return.
