Proposal № 010
The Third Seat

Thursday, September 10, 2026 · Put the factory two-person bench in the single-seat vans · Same bolt holes, one Sunday, target under $500 a van
Bottom line

When a three-man crew rides out in a single-seat van, the third man sits on the floor between the front seats with his back against the partition and nothing holding him in. Ram already built the fix and one of our vans has it: the two-person passenger bench. It bolts into the same four floor holes as the single seat, the middle rider gets a real cushion, a real backrest, and a factory belt, and the driver and passenger seats keep everything they have now. The only real cost is the seat. Dealers won't sell one assembled, but the used market is full of them because camper-van builders pull benches out to put single seats in. Target is under $500 per van and about an hour to swap on a van-reset Sunday.

1 · What we have now

All three vans are Ram ProMaster high-roofs. One came from the factory with the two-person passenger bench. The others have a single passenger seat, so on a three-man day the odd man rides on the floor between the seats, back against the steel partition and the seat rails. No cushion, no belt, and on a long Greenville-to-job run it wears on him. A $50 padded board that sticks to the partition is on the shelf as a stopgap; this proposal replaces the stopgap with the real thing.

2 · What Ram built

Ram never offered a small jump seat for the middle. Their answer was a combined center-and-passenger seat, sold as the 60/40 split bench (parts catalogs call it 40/20/40). You remove the single passenger seat and bolt the bench into its place with no other modification; it is factory equipment, crash-tested and belted for two.

3 · What the middle rider gets, and what he doesn't

He gets a seat and a lap-and-shoulder belt anchored by Ram, which is the whole point. Two things the van's computer won't know about because the middle-seat plugs have nowhere to go: the "buckle up" chime won't nag him, and the middle belt's crash tightener stays unhooked. The belt itself still latches and holds like any belt. The passenger-side airbag and the two outboard belts work exactly as they do today because their three plugs reconnect to the same three sockets.

There is a fair chance the airbag warning light comes on after the swap; one owner who put a bench into a single-seat 2014 saw it, others didn't. It is a known, solved problem on the ProMaster forum (a small resistor on the open plug tells the computer the seat is accounted for) and Ben will sort it the same weekend. If a used seat comes with a spent belt tightener, which is common on salvage seats, the belt buckle assembly is a $65–170 part on eBay and swaps in with two bolts.

4 · Where the seats come from

This is a buyer's market because van conversion people want the opposite trade. Every week someone on the ProMaster forum is trying to get rid of a bench to put in a single seat, and some will pay cash on top to do it. Ranked cheapest to dearest:

  1. Trade with a camper builder. Our single seat for their bench. Forum "trade wanted" posts come from all over, including North Carolina and Georgia; one Boston owner offered his bench plus $400 for a single seat. Best case we come out ahead. Forum classifieds.
  2. Buy outright from a private seller. Recent forum sales: $200 (Bay Area, 2021), $300 (2019), $500 asking for a 2018 (Bellingham). Figure $200–500 and a drive or a freight pallet.
  3. Salvage yard network. car-part.com searches every yard in the country by zip. A forum member priced one at $175 in 2019; expect $200–400 today plus freight if it's not local.
  4. eBay, last resort. Complete OEM benches are listed at $1,150 to $2,250 plus $150–300 freight. That's dealer-adjacent money for a used seat and not where this should land.

6 · How it goes

  1. Jeff says yes to a ceiling. $500 a seat lets Ben close a private sale on the spot instead of losing it to the next guy. Which vans: any single-seat van that runs three-man crews, starting with the one that does it most.
  2. Ben confirms each van's year off the door-jamb sticker so the wanted ad asks for the right generation (2014–2018 or 2019+).
  3. Wanted ads go up on the ProMaster forum classifieds and the ProMaster Facebook groups, offering our single seat in trade, and car-part.com gets searched out to 300 miles. This is a two-week hunt, not a two-day one.
  4. Swap day, on a Sunday. Battery negative off. Unplug three connectors, four bolts out, single seat out. Bench in, four bolts, same three plugs back on, the two middle-seat pigtails tied off. Start the van and read the dash. No light, done. Light on, Ben fits the resistor fix that afternoon.
  5. Second van, same play, with the first swap as the template.

7 · Reality check

The middle seat on a ProMaster is a narrow one. The rider's knees straddle the center of the dash and it's a work seat, not a recliner. It is still a seat with a belt, and on the days it matters that's the whole difference. The one thing that can push this past the ceiling is impatience: if we want a seat this week the answer is eBay at four times the price. If we can hunt for a few weeks, the camper-van crowd will hand us one for close to nothing.
