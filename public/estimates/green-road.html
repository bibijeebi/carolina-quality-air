1 · The job

The City is replacing six packaged units (three on the community center roof, three on the ground) and one 20-year-old split heat pump, about 55 tons on the schedule, and installing new BACnet controls. The existing ductwork stays and reconnects. The bid form is one lump sum plus two alternates with no separate cleaning line; scope item 2 of the base bid is "Clean existing ductwork on existing units," carried inside the prime's lump sum, and the 92-page spec book has no cleaning specification, standard or method. One of the two gyms keeps its four-year-old unit, and Addendum 1 item 6 puts its duct out of the cleaning scope in writing. The other gym is fed from the ground through two insulated risers and distributes through two fabric ducts under the deck, about 25 feet up; Addendum 1 item 5 says they are removed, cleaned and reinstalled. The building will be vacant during construction, so this is daytime work. Air must be moving 30 days from notice to proceed, so the cleaning slots between demo and the new units firing, and the prime will want it done in two days, not stretched over two weeks.

6 · Ben's first pass at the number, for Jeff to correct

Commercial ductwork prices off square footage at $0.90 a foot. Units that stay get cleaned at $500 to $600 each for a 10-ton, more with heating and cooling coils, less with gas or electric heat; here every unit is replaced, so that line is zero. Reheat coils and VAV boxes would add, and this building has neither until Alternate 1 puts new VAVs in, which are new and not ours. The cross-check is the day: commercial should clear about $10,000 per crew-day, and a job like this has to be done fast, two days with three crews, not one man and a helper over two weeks. The $100 per man-hour figure is the floor CQA will not leave the shop for; it is not the price.

Jeff's point on the call: the crew is exposed for two or three days, somebody can get hurt, a floor can crack, and the number has to carry that. $24,000 does. For scale, the base bid likely opens around $300,000 to $400,000 (57 tons of replacements, new curbs and roof duct, controls, test and balance; plus or minus 25 percent). Our line is 6 to 8 percent of that. A prime does not shop a line that size hard; he wants a number that plugs in, a sub who is there when the units are out, and closeout paperwork the City accepts.

7 · Draft quote, as the primes would see it

$24,000 base · two days · daytime · one mobilization · lift included · both fabric ducts laundered

Scope: NADCA ACR source-removal cleaning of all supply and return ductwork served by the six replaced packaged units and the split system, approximately 22,000 square feet of building. Registers and grilles cleaned and reinstalled. Flex branches vacuumed and soft-brushed. Access doors installed per NADCA where cut. Both gym fabric ducts removed in sections, laundered per manufacturer, reinstalled in order, both metal-to-fabric collars cleaned and resealed. Before and after photos and a closeout report for the contractor's submittal.

Excluded: X-RTU-4 ductwork, the retained gym, per Addendum 1 item 6. Coil cleaning, all units are new. Ductwork replaced under the base bid (RTU-2 and RTU-3 roof penetrations).

Adjustments: Alternate 1 accepted, deduct $4,000 (RTU-1 distribution is replaced; about 4,500 square feet of office, weight room, meeting room and lobby come out). Lift provided by the mechanical contractor, deduct $800. Additional mobilization beyond the first, $850 each.

Encapsulation, unit price: if degraded internal fiberglass liner is found during cleaning, encapsulate per NADCA at $__ per linear foot, photo-documented, by change order. Jeff sets the rate. The risers are externally insulated, so liner is most likely on the older rectangular trunks above the community center ceilings, if anywhere.

Terms: quote valid 60 days. Certificate of insurance naming the contractor and the City as additional insured on award. Payment net 30 from the contractor's receipt of payment for our portion; retainage, if passed down, released with the contractor's. Our two days land inside the first 60 days of the contract times. Excludes permits, coil cleaning, duct replaced under the base bid, and the retained gym unless added.

The scope language is the product. Nobody else has a cleaning spec to work from, so the first clean, NADCA-worded scope a prime receives is the one he carries. The fabric duct line and the lift-both-ways line are the tell that we walked it. The only other duct cleaner on the plan holders list, ChuteMaster Environmental of New Jersey, was not in the room; priced off the documents alone they miss the fabric duct, and priced right they carry two trips, two hotel stays and about 16 paid windshield hours before they clean a foot of duct. The more likely competition is a prime self-performing a rough cleaning with his own guys, which the NADCA question below is designed to take off the table.
