Jeff,

Following our call and the walkthrough, I’m proposing $24,000 for the retained ductwork and both gym fabric ducts, including lift rental and fabric cleaning. The working plan is me, Devin and Marcus, with you helping, targeting two site days. We still need to confirm that the fabric turnaround and access allow that schedule.

This fits the $20,000–$25,000 range you described as good money for a two-day commercial job. Before we send it, I want to settle the scope below, make sure the crew and equipment plan work, and get your final number.

01 / The work

What we would cover

  • Clean the retained supply and return ductwork serving RTU-1, RTU-2, RTU-3, AC-5, AC-6, AC-7 and AHU-8, following NADCA ACR.
  • Remove, identify, clean and reinstall both gym fabric ducts using the manufacturer’s instructions. Include handling, transport, drying and reconnection in the work plan.
  • Clean associated retained grilles and registers, close cleaning access openings, and provide before-and-after documentation.
  • Include the lift and appropriate floor protection for the fabric work.

Excluded & alternate work

The retained gym’s ductwork is excluded by Addendum 1. The base bid also replaces flex, diffusers and balancing dampers for RTU-1, RTU-2, RTU-3 and AC-5; we would clean the retained distribution, not charge to clean components being discarded.

Equipment replacement, new duct installation, controls, and testing and balancing remain with the mechanical contractor and its other trades. Coating or repairs would be separately assessed and approved if needed.

If Alternate 1 is accepted, RTU-1 distribution is replaced. We need a cleaning deduct for the work that actually disappears.

02 / Price basis

What changed since our call

The 30,000-square-foot figure I gave you was a rough whole-building estimate. M-1 includes room areas, and the retained gym is outside our duct-cleaning scope. The ventilation-schedule entries suggest roughly 16,100–17,400 square feet serving the remaining systems, depending on how the RTU-2/3 room entry is counted. That is a cross-check, not a completed takeoff.

I’m therefore proposing $24,000 as a job price for the scope and crew plan, rather than presenting it as a verified square-foot calculation. We should check that it carries our labor, lift, fabric handling, travel and risk without counting the same work twice.

Awaiting clarification · 2 questions

Two answers pending from Brad

  1. Fabric ducts: manufacturer and care information so we can confirm the cleaning process and turnaround.
  2. Retained unit: confirmation that X-RTU-4 is X-AC-4 on the drawings and that its coil cleaning and new filters remain required. The drawings call for this even though its ducts are excluded. The proposed base above does not include that equipment work; once confirmed, we can price it separately or clearly leave it with the prime.

Neither answer prevents us from settling the rest of the estimate now.

03 / Next steps

Timing and release

The minutes schedule the final addendum by September 11. Once we review it, resolve the two pending answers and have your price, the target is to get the quote to confirmed mechanical bidders September 14–15, or earlier if they need it. Their City bids are due September 22 at 1 PM.

Our cleaning needs to fit the prime’s sequence before the 30-day air-moving milestone; the 60-day milestone is for controls. The building is expected to be vacant, with access to be coordinated.

Once approved, we can prepare one consistent quote for Becky to issue through the usual process.
