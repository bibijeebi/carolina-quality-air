1 · The job in one paragraph

The City is replacing six packaged units (three on the community center roof, three on the ground) and one 20-year-old split, 57 tons, and installing new BACnet controls. The existing ductwork stays and reconnects. Base bid item 2 is "Clean existing ductwork on existing units," and the 92-page spec book has no cleaning specification, no standard, no method. One of the two gyms keeps its four-year-old unit and is out of the replacement scope; the engineer said its duct "shouldn't be cleaned." The other gym is fed from the ground through two insulated risers and distributes through two fabric ducts under the deck, about 25 feet up, which the engineer said twice are to be taken down and laundered, not replaced. The building will be vacant during construction. Air must be moving 30 days from notice to proceed, so the cleaning slots between demo and the new units firing.

4 · The quote as the primes will see it

$12,400 base · daytime · one mobilization · lift included · both fabric ducts laundered

Scope: NADCA ACR source-removal cleaning of all supply and return ductwork served by the six replaced packaged units and the split system. Registers and grilles cleaned and reinstalled. Flex branches vacuumed and soft-brushed. Access doors installed per NADCA where cut. Both gym fabric ducts removed in sections, laundered per manufacturer, reinstalled in order, both metal-to-fabric collars cleaned and resealed. Before and after photos and a closeout report for the contractor's submittal.

Excluded: the gym with the four-year-old unit. Coil cleaning, all units are new. Ductwork replaced under the base bid (4-A and 4-B roof penetrations).

Adjustments: Alternate 1 accepted, deduct $1,100 (RTU-1 distribution is replaced). Retained gym added by addendum, add $1,800. Lift provided by the mechanical contractor, deduct $700. Additional mobilization beyond the first, $850 each (a two-man trip is four door-to-door man-hours before setup).

Encapsulation, unit price: if degraded internal fiberglass liner is found during cleaning, encapsulate per NADCA at $__ per linear foot, photo-documented, by change order. Jeff sets the rate. This 1970s-80s block building with painted rectangular trunks is the vintage that has liner, and it is the line that turns a cleaning job into a real one.

Terms: quote valid 60 days. Certificate of insurance naming the contractor and the City as additional insured on award. Payment net 30 from the contractor's receipt of payment for our portion. Excludes permits, coil cleaning, duct replaced under the base bid, and the retained gym unless added.

The scope language is the product. Nobody else has a cleaning spec to work from, so the first clean, NADCA-worded scope a prime receives is the one he carries in his bid. The fabric duct line and the lift-both-ways line are the tell that we walked it, and the lift line makes us cheaper than the out-of-state competitor the moment a prime shares the lift he already has in that gym.

The competition

The only other duct cleaner on the plan holders list is ChuteMaster Environmental out of New Jersey. Nobody from ChuteMaster was at the pre-bid as far as Ben could tell; the room was about a dozen companies, all of them local, primes plus controls, test-and-balance and electrical subs. If ChuteMaster quotes off the documents alone they will miss the fabric duct, and if they price it right they carry two trips from New Jersey, two hotel stays and roughly 16 paid windshield hours, $2,000 to $2,500 of cost before they clean a foot of duct. For us it is two 20-minute drives. The more likely competition is a prime self-performing a rough cleaning with his own guys, which the NADCA ACR question is designed to take off the table.
