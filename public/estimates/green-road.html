Jeff's method: count the crew-days off the walk, price the day, put the adders in the bid instead of eating them. The rates below are Ben's guesses at CQA's real numbers.

Labor. Assumed commercial crew-day rate of $2,400 to $3,000 loaded. Three to three and a half days puts labor at $7,200 to $10,500.
Adders that belong in the bid. Scissor lift for the gyms, two days, $700 to $900 rental unless we own one. After-hours work if the center stays open during construction, plus 15% on labor, about $1,100 to $1,500. Each mobilization beyond the first, $600 to $750, because the prime may want the cleaning done roof by roof as the units are swapped.
Cross-checks. Per system: seven units at $1,200 to $1,500 each is $8,400 to $10,500. Per square foot: $0.30 to $0.37 on 30,000, low end of commercial and right for a building that is two-thirds open gym. Tech-hour: three men, 32 hours, at $110 to $120 is $10,500 to $11,500. All three land in the same band.

4 · Proposed quote to the primes

$9,400 base · daytime · one mobilization · lift included

Scope: NADCA ACR source-removal cleaning of all supply and return ductwork served by the seven units. Registers and grilles cleaned and reinstalled. Access doors installed per NADCA where cut. Before and after photos and a closeout report for the prime's submittal.

Lines under it: additional mobilization $650 each. After-hours work plus 15%. No coil cleaning, all units are new.

The scope language is the real product. The spec book has none, and the first clean, NADCA-worded scope a prime receives is the one he carries in his bid. That makes our number the reference number for the line, not one of several.

Why we beat ChuteMaster without cutting margin

They are in New Jersey. Three men, three nights at $150 a head, plus roughly 16 paid hours of windshield, is $2,000 to $2,500 of cost they carry before they clean a foot of duct. We are 20 minutes from the Cary office. Same crew-days, no travel stack.

5 · What the walk answers

  1. Open or closed during construction? Open means nights and the 15% adder. That is the single biggest swing.
  2. Gym ceiling height and duct type. Over 14 feet needs a lift, a $900 swing. Spiral or rectangular, lined or bare.
  3. How the prime phases the units. All seven at once or roof by roof over weeks. One trip or three.
  4. Where the vac truck parks for Gym 2 and how far the hose runs to the wall penetration.
  5. Where the seventh unit is. The split system is not visible from outside.
  6. On the record: is there a cleaning standard the city wants, NADCA ACR? If it lands in an addendum, every prime needs a NADCA sub, and there are two on the list.
