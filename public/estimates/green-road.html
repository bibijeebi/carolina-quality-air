1 · What the walk changed

Building vacant. Engineer said to bid as if the facility is closed and the contractor has the run of it. Daytime work. The after-hours adder is gone.
One gym out. The base bid replaces everything "less this gym," the one whose unit was replaced four years ago. The engineer said its duct "shouldn't be cleaned" and will clarify by addendum. Quote it as excluded by name, with an add price if the addendum says otherwise.
Fabric duct in the in-scope gym. Engineer's words: not replacing the duct sock, take it down and launder it, clean all the other duct. That is a lift day to drop and rehang, commercial laundering per the manufacturer, and cleaning the metal risers and the metal-to-fabric collar, which is visibly dirty.
4-A and 4-B get new curbs. The two 3.5-ton side-shot units on rails get new roof curbs and new duct through the roof in the base bid, so that first section is new. Cleaning starts below the deck.
Alternate 1 replaces RTU-1's distribution. The 10-ton over the office, weight room, meeting room and lobby gets new VAV terminals and ductwork if Alt 1 is accepted. Our quote carries a deduct for that duct so it matches the bid form.
Schedule is tight. Air moving 30 days from notice to proceed, conditioning by 60. The cleaning has to slot between demo and the new units firing, which favors one or two mobilizations alongside the mechanical crew, not spread out.
What is written is gospel. The engineer closed by saying nothing he said on the walk is binding until it is in an addendum. Questions in writing to brad.w.felts@imegcorp.com by close of business Wed Sept 9, last addendum Sept 11.

5 · Proposed quote to the primes, revised

$9,200 base · daytime · one mobilization · lift included

Scope: NADCA ACR source-removal cleaning of all supply and return ductwork served by the six replaced packaged units and the split system. Registers and grilles cleaned and reinstalled. Flex branches vacuumed and soft-brushed. Access doors installed per NADCA where cut. Gym fabric duct removed, laundered per manufacturer, reinstalled, and the metal-to-fabric collar cleaned and resealed. Before and after photos and a closeout report for the prime's submittal.

Excluded: the gym with the four-year-old unit (add $1,800 if the addendum puts it in scope). Coil cleaning, all units are new. Duct replaced under the base bid (4-A and 4-B roof penetrations).

Lines under it: Alternate 1 accepted, deduct $1,100 (RTU-1 distribution is replaced). Additional mobilization $650 each.

The scope language is the real product. The spec book has none, and the first clean, NADCA-worded scope a prime receives is the one he carries. The fabric duct line is the tell that we walked it.

Why we beat ChuteMaster without cutting margin

They are in New Jersey. Three men, three nights at $150 a head, plus roughly 16 paid hours of windshield, is $2,000 to $2,500 of cost they carry before they clean a foot of duct. We are 20 minutes from the Cary office, and the fabric duct needs two trips, which is two hotel stays for them and none for us.
