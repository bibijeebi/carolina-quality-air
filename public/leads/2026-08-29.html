1 · The restoration sub lane

Every franchise is its own buyer with its own project manager, so Servpro Greenville and Servpro North Raleigh are two relationships, not one. What a PM needs to hear is three things: CQA goes anywhere (Richmond, DC), fields 4–5 crews and does 20,000 sq ft in two days, and answers the phone. That is a capacity pitch.

2 · Where the facilities crowd meets

What Perry does informally has two formal versions in the Triangle, and both let suppliers in.

Facility managersIFMA Greater Triangle Chapter
The facility managers’ club: hospitals, campuses, corporate sites, school plants. Building tours with a social after (the Lenovo Center tour, then drinks at The Local), brewery socials, monthly education meetings. Suppliers join as Associate members, and the chapter caps associate seats at events, which means the FMs go partly to meet vendors. Chapter admin: Candi McDowell, 919-863-8057. First move: ask to attend one meeting as a guest before joining.
Building owners & managersBOMA Raleigh-Durham
Commercial office, medical, industrial owners and property managers. More suits than boots. Same play: guest first, then decide.
InformalWhere the maintenance guys actually are
Supply house counters at 7 AM (Johnstone, Ferguson, Grainger). Hospital and university plant operations offices. School district maintenance shops. County facilities departments. Nobody cold-calls those people about ducts. Someone who shows up with the Ronald McDonald House before-and-after on a phone and says “call me when you spot bare metal on a supply” is remembered.

Question for Jeff: where does Perry actually run into these guys? That answer beats both associations.

3 · Every lined-duct-era commercial building in Wake County
